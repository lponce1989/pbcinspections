Citizens eligibility: the 20% rule, the Clearinghouse and the inspections you need

Checked Nov 18, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: under Florida Statutes s. 627.351(6)(c)5., a primary residence applying to Citizens Property Insurance is not eligible if an authorized insurer offers coverage at a premium that is not more than 20 percent greater than Citizens' premium for comparable coverage. Citizens says you may be eligible if no private-market companies are willing to offer coverage at their approved rates, or if private-market offers are more than 20% higher than comparable Citizens coverage. Citizens also requires a 4-point inspection for applications on properties more than 20 years old, and its agent guide lists mitigation credits among the items a comparable quote must match.

Citizens' agent guide describes the company as providing property insurance to people who are in good faith entitled to obtain coverage through the private market but are unable to do so, and says agents must establish eligibility before placing a policy with Citizens. This guide covers how that eligibility test works, the limits on which homes Citizens can insure, and the inspection reports that go with an application. For the inspections themselves, see 4-point vs. wind mitigation vs. roof certification.

The two ways to qualify

Citizens' agent guide lists two routes for personal lines coverage, and says the named insured and the property must also meet all other underwriting criteria:

  • No offer of coverage: the applicant is unaware of any offer of coverage from any authorized insurer.
  • The 20% rule: the premium for all offers of coverage made by authorized insurers is more than 20% greater than the premium for comparable coverage from Citizens.

Citizens announced that the threshold went from 15% to 20% for personal lines new business effective July 1, 2021, to comply with Senate Bill 76. The statute says the 20 percent test applies to an offer at the insurer's approved rate under a standard policy including wind coverage, or a basic policy including wind coverage if that is consistent with the insurer's filed underwriting rules.

What counts as a comparable quote

Citizens' agent guide says a comparable quote must be created before a new-business submission when the policyholder has a private-market offer, and that these items must match the private-market offer:

  • Coverage A limits
  • Hurricane deductibles
  • Ordinance and law coverage percentage, if offered
  • Mitigation credits
  • Loss settlement (actual cash value or replacement cost)

The guide says the remaining coverages should be the same, and that the closest available option should be presented when carrier options are not an exact match. Under s. 627.351(6)(c)5., if Citizens or the applicant asks the private insurer for a breakdown of the offer's premium by type of coverage and the insurer refuses or is unable to provide it, Citizens may treat the offer as not being an offer of coverage at the insurer's approved rate. For how hurricane deductibles and mitigation credits work, see Florida hurricane deductibles and your wind mitigation and how much a wind mitigation inspection saves.

How the Clearinghouse works

Florida Statutes s. 627.3518 requires Citizens to run a personal lines clearinghouse to confirm an applicant's eligibility, give new applicants and existing policyholders access to offers from authorized insurers, and divert ineligible applicants and policyholders into the voluntary market. The statute requires all new applications for personal lines coverage, and all personal lines policies due for renewal, to be submitted to the program before Citizens binds or renews coverage.

  • Which policies go through it: Citizens' Clearinghouse page says agents use the Clearinghouse to quote HO-3, HO-6, DP-1-Dwelling and DP-3-Dwelling policies, and use Citizens' PolicyCenter system for HO-4, HO-8, DP-3-C, DP-1-C, MHO-3, MHO-4, MDP-1 and all wind-only policy types, where the agent determines eligibility, for example with a comparative rater.
  • Proof of prior coverage: the same page says agents may be required to upload a renewal offer, a nonrenewal notice, a cancellation notice or new purchase documents, and that no proof is needed if coverage lapsed for over 45 days.
  • Proof of the offers: it also lists a screenshot of a comparative rater, or at least one quote from a carrier's website, showing offers more than 20% greater than the Citizens premium, or a screenshot or declination showing that no offers are available, with coverages matching the final policy.
  • Offers above the threshold: under s. 627.3518(6)(a), if a Clearinghouse offer from an authorized insurer exceeds the eligibility threshold, a new applicant may accept that coverage or accept coverage with Citizens, and a renewing policyholder may accept it or continue with Citizens.

What happens at renewal

For a primary residence, s. 627.351(6)(c)5. says that for policies renewing on or after April 1, 2023, the home is not eligible to stay with Citizens unless the private insurer's premium is more than 20 percent greater than Citizens' renewal premium for comparable coverage. Citizens' Clearinghouse page says agents must remarket all Citizens business at renewal, and that any offer not more than 20% greater than the Citizens renewal premium will result in a nonrenewal of the Citizens policy, after which a new-business policy will need to be written with another carrier.

  • Policies outside the Clearinghouse: for HO-4, HO-8, DP-3-C, DP-1-C, MHO-3, MHO-4, MDP-1 and all wind-only forms, Citizens says agents must remarket each policy at renewal with the carriers they can quote and write, and contact the insured if a private-market offer is available.
  • Choosing to leave: the same page says agents can submit a quote to a private-market carrier any time a valid offer is present, including when a policy is eligible to renew with Citizens but the policyholder wishes to accept coverage at a greater premium.
  • Takeouts: the statute says a policyholder removed from Citizens through an assumption agreement does not remain eligible for Citizens coverage after the end of the policy term. For the notice rules on takeout nonrenewals, see insurer inspection found problems: repairs, cancellation and nonrenewal. For how takeout offers, opt-outs and choice deadlines work, see Citizens takeout offers.

Second homes and rentals

Citizens' agent guide notes that a personal lines non-primary residence will not be eligible for Citizens if an approved surplus lines insurer, as part of its take-out plan, offers comparable coverage not more than 20% greater than Citizens' premium. For a non-primary residence renewing on or after July 1, 2024, s. 627.351(6)(c)5. applies the same more-than-20-percent test to renewal offers from an authorized insurer at its approved rate or from an approved surplus lines insurer at the rate approved as part of its take-out plan. Landlords can find the inspection rules for rentals in rental property inspections in Florida.

Value and opening protection limits

  • $700,000: under s. 627.351(6)(a)3., effective Jan 1, 2017, a structure with a dwelling replacement cost of $700,000 or more, or a single condominium unit with a combined dwelling and contents replacement cost of $700,000 or more, is not eligible for Citizens coverage.
  • Counties without competition: the same subparagraph says that limit does not apply in counties where the Office of Insurance Regulation determines there is not a reasonable degree of competition, and that in those counties a structure or condo unit under $1 million is eligible.
  • Opening protection at $750,000: under s. 627.351(6)(a)5.a., effective Jan 1, 2009, a personal lines residential structure in the wind-borne debris region with an insured value on the structure of $750,000 or more is not eligible unless it has the opening protections the Florida Building Code requires for a newly built home in that area. The statute says a home complies if it has shutters or opening protections on all openings and those protections complied with the Florida Building Code when they were installed. For how opening protection is graded on the wind mitigation form, see impact windows and shutters on the wind mitigation form.

The inspections Citizens asks for

Citizens' inspections page says it may require certain inspections depending on the type and amount of coverage, eligible discounts, and the age and location of the property, and that the property owner pays for some inspections while Citizens pays for others.

  • 4-point: Citizens requires a four-point inspection for all property owner, dwelling and mobile home applications for properties more than 20 years old. A Florida-licensed inspector checks the electrical, plumbing, heating and cooling (HVAC) systems and the roof, and Citizens says serious hazards or deficiencies, such as exposed wiring, active leaks or evidence of leaks, should be repaired before an application is submitted. See common 4-point inspection problems.
  • Older roofs: Citizens says roofs older than 25 years (soft, such as shingle) or 50 years (hard, such as tile, slate, clay, concrete or metal) require documentation showing at least five years of remaining useful life, and the inspector completes a Roof Inspection Form. If the roof has less than five years left, Citizens says the owner must provide proof of a full roof replacement to the inspector before any policy is written. See Citizens' Roof Inspection Form (RCF-1).
  • Wind mitigation: Citizens says homes with wind-damage mitigation features may qualify for premium discounts by documenting approved features through a wind mitigation inspection.
  • Field surveys: Citizens may also require an exterior-only or interior and exterior underwriting field survey, free to the policyholder and done by a third-party field survey company, as a condition for new coverage or during the life of the policy.

Citizens' agent guide says the agent enters the four-point inspection date on the application if it is required based on year built, enters the roof's remaining useful life from the four-point report if the year of the last roofing update is unacceptable under Citizens' guidelines, and enters wind-loss mitigation information where it applies, with the inspector's details for each inspection. Its Clearinghouse page adds that homes submitted to private-market carriers are subject to all new-business underwriting and documentation requirements, which can include current inspections. FAQ

What is the Citizens 20% rule?

Under s. 627.351(6)(c)5., a primary residence is not eligible for Citizens if an authorized insurer offers coverage at a premium that is not more than 20 percent greater than Citizens' premium for comparable coverage. Citizens says you may be eligible if no private-market company will offer coverage at its approved rates, or if every private-market offer is more than 20% higher.

Does the 20% rule apply when my Citizens policy renews?

Yes. For primary residences renewing on or after April 1, 2023, the statute says the home is not eligible to stay unless the private offer is more than 20 percent greater than Citizens' renewal premium. Citizens says an offer not more than 20% greater than the renewal premium will result in a nonrenewal.

What has to match for a quote to be comparable?

Citizens' agent guide lists Coverage A limits, hurricane deductibles, the ordinance and law percentage if offered, mitigation credits, and loss settlement (actual cash value or replacement cost).

Is there a value limit for Citizens?

Yes. Under s. 627.351(6)(a)3., a structure with a dwelling replacement cost of $700,000 or more, or a condo unit with combined dwelling and contents replacement cost of $700,000 or more, is not eligible, except in counties where the Office of Insurance Regulation finds there is not a reasonable degree of competition.

Do I need a 4-point inspection to apply to Citizens?

Citizens says it requires a four-point inspection for all property owner, dwelling and mobile home applications for properties more than 20 years old.

My Safe Florida Home grant: who qualifies and the steps to payment

Checked Nov 13, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: under Florida Statutes s. 215.5586, the free My Safe Florida Home (MSFH) inspection is for site-built, owner-occupied single-family homes with a homestead exemption. A grant also requires an MSFH initial inspection within the prior 24 months, an insured value of $700,000 or less, a home built before January 1, 2008, and a low- or moderate-income household. Grants are matched $1 from you for every $2 from the state, up to $10,000 from the state; low-income applicants can get up to $10,000 with no match. Work done before your grant is approved is not paid.

This guide covers eligibility and the grant steps. For how the program's free inspection compares with paying a private inspector, see My Safe Florida Home vs. a private wind mitigation.

Who can get the free inspection

Section 215.5586(1)(a) says an applicant is eligible for a program hurricane mitigation inspection if all of these are true:

  • The home is a single-family unit on its own parcel of land, either detached or attached and no more than three stories.
  • The home is site-built and owner-occupied.
  • You have been granted a homestead exemption on the home under chapter 196.

The program's Homeowner's Guide (2025-2026) says retirement homes, mobile and manufactured homes, and second homes, vacation homes and rental properties are not eligible for the free initial inspection, and that attached single-family homes are treated as townhouses. If you own a mobile home or a rental, see mobile home credits and tie-downs or rental property inspections.

The guide says the program uses your county Property Appraiser's data to check your homestead exemption and your property's use code, and that homes with a use code beginning with "1" or "01" are considered single-family. In Palm Beach County, that office is the Palm Beach County Property Appraiser. Under s. 215.5586(1)(c), you can get the inspection without being eligible for a grant or applying for one.

Who can get a grant

Section 215.5586(2)(a) lists the grant criteria. You must:

  • Be eligible for the inspection above.
  • Have a dwelling with an insured value of $700,000 or less.
  • Have had an initial inspection through the program within the 24 months before you apply.
  • Have a home built before January 1, 2008, as shown on the county property appraiser's website.
  • Agree to a final inspection when the project is done.
  • Agree to give the department what your insurer tells you about discounts from the funded improvements.
  • Be a low-income or moderate-income person as defined in s. 420.0004.

Section 420.0004 defines low-income as household income no more than 80 percent of the median, and moderate-income as less than 120 percent of the median, using the state median or the median for your metropolitan area or county, whichever is greater. The Homeowner's Guide points applicants to HUD's income limits page to check where they fall.

On the build date, the program's sources use slightly different wording. The statute and the program's support center say the home must have been built before January 1, 2008, as reflected on the county property appraiser's website. The Homeowner's Guide says the home's initial building construction permit must have been issued before January 1, 2008, and in another section that the building permit application must have been made before that date. The guide says to contact your county Property Appraiser or Tax Collector for information about your home's building permit; see also how to find your permits in Palm Beach County.

For the insured value, the program's support center says you must submit a declarations page for a currently effective DF-1, HO-1, HO-2, HO-3, HO-5, HO-6 or HO-8 policy that shows the dwelling value under Coverage A.

The five priority groups

The first step is a Prioritization Questionnaire that asks for your household members and household income, among other details. The Homeowner's Guide says it sorts you into one of five groups, and that you cannot be moved from your group once sorted:

  • Group 1: low income, age 60 or older
  • Group 2: low income, age 59 or younger
  • Group 3: moderate income, age 60 or older
  • Group 4: moderate income, age 59 or younger
  • Group 5: all ages, above the moderate-income threshold. Group 5 applicants are not eligible for a grant.

Section 215.5586(2)(i) sets that order and has the program open applications to the first group, then to the later groups 15, 30 and 45 days after it starts accepting applications, and to all other applicants after 60 days. The guide says that if the program runs out of funding before your group opens, you will not be able to apply for an inspection or a grant.

The steps, from questionnaire to check

The Homeowner's Guide lists 10 steps:

  1. Complete the Prioritization Questionnaire in the Applicant Portal.
  2. Submit an Inspection Application.
  3. Schedule the initial inspection once the application is approved.
  4. Receive the Initial Inspection Report.
  5. Indicate interest in a grant (you must opt in to get the Grant Application).
  6. Submit the Grant Application.
  7. Complete the project with your contractor, based on the report's recommendations.
  8. Schedule the final inspection.
  9. Receive the Final Inspection Report.
  10. Submit a Draw Request to be paid.

The guide says you cannot choose the inspector: after approval, the program emails you the assigned inspector's name and the inspector contacts you within seven days to schedule. It says the Initial Inspection Report includes recommended improvements, cost estimates, a summary of the OIR-B1-1802 and the OIR-B1-1802 form itself. Applicants with no recommended improvements, or in Group 5, cannot go on to the grant phase.

The program's home page, checked Nov 13, 2026, says it offers free wind mitigation inspections and grants of up to $10,000, and that homeowners are sorted into inspection groups 1 to 5 several times each week. Section 215.5586 says the program depends on annual legislative appropriations and does not create an entitlement.

How much the grant pays

Section 215.5586(2)(c) says grants are matched $1 from the applicant for every $2 from the state, up to a maximum state contribution of $10,000. The Homeowner's Guide calls these Matching Grants and says they reimburse two-thirds of the project cost, paid after you submit a paid-in-full invoice. Its own examples:

$9,000$6,000$3,000
$15,000$10,000$5,000
$20,000$10,000 (the maximum)$10,000

Under s. 215.5586(2)(h), low-income applicants can get a grant of up to $10,000 without a match. The guide says the Low-Income Grant is paid directly to you, does not require a paid-in-full invoice, and that you are responsible for paying the contractor.

What the grant can pay for

Grants can be used only for improvements recommended by the initial inspection (s. 215.5586(2)(e)). The Homeowner's Guide lists four:

  • Opening protection: impact-rated windows or shutters, exterior doors and garage doors. You don't have to replace every opening, only ones recommended in your report. If you already have impact-rated shutters, the guide says a request for impact windows will be denied, because the grant cannot swap one type of opening protection for another. See how the form rates windows and shutters.
  • Roof-to-wall attachment: clips, wraps or added nails, done to every truss or rafter. See toe nails, clips and wraps.
  • Roof deck attachment: more or longer nails holding the roof sheathing to the trusses or rafters. See 6d vs. 8d nails.
  • Secondary water resistance: full peel-and-stick underlayment or seam tape when reroofing, or foam adhesive on the seams from the attic. See what counts as a sealed roof deck.

The guide says townhouses, including attached single-family homes, can get funding only for opening protection. If the contractor must remove the roof covering to do a recommended improvement, the covering's replacement can be included in the project cost, but the whole contiguous roof must be replaced; patching and partial repairs are not funded. It also says a double layer of felt or synthetic underlayment with no tape can be funded as a secondary water barrier, with the scope shown on the contractor's invoices. More on reroof rules in new roof and your wind mitigation.

Rules that can cost you the grant

  • Don't start early: the guide says performing work, signing with a contractor or buying materials before you get the program's email to proceed will result in a denial.
  • Use a licensed contractor: the grant application must name the intended contractor and state license number, and the program verifies it (s. 215.5586(2)(b)). The guide says the license must be certified or registered with DBPR and that checking it is your responsibility; you can search on DBPR's license search.
  • Get permits: work must have all required local permits and inspections and be done by properly licensed contractors (s. 215.5586(2)(d)). The guide says to make sure permits are closed out before you request the final inspection.
  • Finish everything first: the guide allows one final inspection per applicant, and if you are doing more than one improvement, all of them must be finished before you request it. Only improvements recommended in the initial report and observed in the final report are paid.
  • Watch the clock: the guide says to request the final inspection, or a six-month extension, within one year of grant approval. Section 215.5586(2)(j) gives 18 months after approval to finish and request the final inspection, or the application is deemed abandoned.
  • Answer requests: the guide says requests for information must be answered within 60 days or the application is closed.
  • Check the mailing address: the guide says checks cannot be delivered to an active USPS forwarding address, and to allow at least three weeks for the check after approval.

The insurance discount step

The Final Inspection Report includes an OIR-B1-1802 signed by the inspector and you. Before you can submit the Draw Request, the guide says you must send the Final Inspection Report to your insurer and ask about discounts, then upload a new declarations page, or a letter or email from the insurer showing the premium change or saying no discount will apply. The support center says you do not need to receive a discount to be paid, but you must submit the insurer's response. For how the form gets to your insurer, see sending your wind mitigation report.

If you don't qualify

A private wind mitigation inspection does not count for the grant: the program's support center says work must be based on the recommendations in the MSFH Initial Inspection Report. If your home isn't eligible, our Palm Beach County prices page lists private inspection prices published by inspectors, and the city pages list inspectors by area.

FAQ

Who qualifies for a My Safe Florida Home grant?

Under s. 215.5586, you must qualify for the program's inspection (a site-built, owner-occupied single-family home with a homestead exemption), have had an MSFH initial inspection within the prior 24 months, have an insured value of $700,000 or less and a home built before January 1, 2008, and be a low- or moderate-income person as defined in s. 420.0004.

How much does the My Safe Florida Home grant pay?

Section 215.5586 says grants are matched $1 from the applicant for every $2 from the state, up to $10,000 from the state. Low-income applicants can get up to $10,000 with no match.

Can I start the work before my grant is approved?

No. The program's Homeowner's Guide says performing work, signing with a contractor or buying materials before you get the program's email to proceed will result in a denial.

Can I use my own private wind mitigation inspection for the grant?

No. The program's support center says work must be based on the recommended improvements in the MSFH Initial Inspection Report acquired through the program.

Can I get the free inspection if my income is too high for a grant?

Section 215.5586 lets an eligible applicant get the inspection without being eligible for a grant. The Homeowner's Guide puts applicants above the moderate-income threshold in Group 5, which is not eligible for a grant.

Do I have to get an insurance discount to be paid?

No. The program's support center says you do not need a discount to receive payment, but you must submit your insurer's response, even if no discount is applied.

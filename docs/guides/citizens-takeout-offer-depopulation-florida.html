Citizens takeout offers: depopulation, the 20% rule and your deadline

Checked Nov 22, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: a takeout, which Citizens calls depopulation, is when a private insurer approved by the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation (OIR) asks to assume your Citizens policy. Citizens' program guide says it mails or emails a Depopulation Packet about 50 days before the assumption, with each offer's estimated renewal premium and coverage worksheets. Under s. 627.351(6)(ii)3., a primary residence that gets a takeout offer is no longer eligible for Citizens unless the offer's premium is more than 20 percent greater than Citizens' renewal premium for comparable coverage. If you are eligible and want to stay, you must register that choice by the deadline on your Offer Form. If no choice is registered, Citizens assigns the policy to the takeout company with the lowest estimated premium.

How a takeout works

Citizens' takeout infographic says authorized private-market companies may request to take out any active Citizens policy, and that takeouts can occur any time during a policy term. It lays out the steps:

  1. Private-market companies ask OIR for approval to remove policies from Citizens.
  2. OIR reviews the companies' financials, current policies and requested takeout policies. If it approves, it sets a maximum number of Citizens policies each company can remove.
  3. Each company selects the policies it wants. Multiple companies can select the same policy, and the policyholder can choose between the offers.
  4. Citizens mails the Depopulation Packet with all available offers, estimated renewal premiums and coverage worksheets.
  5. Policies are assumed when an offer was selected or no choice was registered, and Citizens sends confirmation letters.

OIR's take-out companies page says approval comes as a consent order showing the number of policies eligible for removal, the assumption date and any other stipulations. OIR's participation requirements ask each company for a depopulation plan that includes its underwriting criteria for the assumed policies, a county-by-county breakdown of the policies requested, and an updated coverage worksheet and coverage comparison. Under s. 627.351(6)(x)2., an authorized insurer considering a risk Citizens insures can be given the relevant underwriting files if it agrees in writing, notarized and under oath, to keep them confidential.

Two kinds of packet

  • Policyholder Choice Offer (eligible to stay): Citizens' sample letter says you received at least one offer from an OIR-approved private insurer and can accept an offer or remain with Citizens. It warns that if you do not register a choice by the due date, Citizens will select a private-market offer for you.
  • Policyholder Depopulation Offer (not eligible to stay): the sample letter says at least one offer is not more than 20% higher than Citizens' estimated renewal premium for comparable coverage, so the policy "is not eligible for renewal with Citizens." Citizens is not listed as a choice, and the form shows what your Citizens estimated renewal premium would have been.

The statute's 20 percent takeout rule in s. 627.351(6)(ii)3. applies only to a policy that covers a primary residence, and to takeout offers in applications to participate in depopulation submitted to OIR on or after Jan 1, 2023. Citizens' program guide says a personal lines non-primary residence is not eligible for Citizens if an approved surplus lines insurer, as part of its take-out plan, offers comparable coverage not more than 20% greater than Citizens' premium. For how the same 20% test works when you first apply, see who qualifies for Citizens under the 20% rule.

Registering your choice or opting out

  • Two ways to register: Citizens' Personal Lines depopulation page says you can have your agent submit your choice or register it online. The Offer Form points to citizensfla.com/online-choice, where you enter your policy number and the 10-digit registration code printed on the form.
  • Time to decide: the program guide says agents and policyholders will have at least 30 days to register a choice.
  • No changes after you register: the program guide says a registered choice cannot be changed, and that the agent or policyholder must contact Citizens Customer Care before the submission deadline if an error was made.
  • Confirmation: Citizens' page says that if you accept an offer online and enter your email, you get a confirmation email. If you skip the email or your agent registers for you, you will not receive a confirmation email or letter. If you choose to stay with Citizens and do not enter an email, or your agent registers for you, Citizens sends a confirmation letter.
  • Missing the deadline: regardless of eligibility, Citizens assigns the policy to the private-market company that offered the lowest premium.
  • No 30-day return: Citizens says, "There no longer is a 30-day post-assumption period during which you can return to Citizens. Once an assumption occurs, the policy transfer is final."
  • More offers later: Citizens says you may receive future assumption offers and must respond to each one to remain with Citizens. It adds that Florida law requires continued validation of Citizens eligibility through renewal remarketing, and that rejecting an offer of assumption does not exempt you from it.

The next assumption dates

Citizens' 2027 calendar lists six personal lines assumption dates, shown in the table above. For the first one, Feb 16, 2027, it shows Depopulation Packets going out Dec 30, 2026 and a Policyholder Choice deadline of Feb 5, 2027. The calendar's notes say OIR approves each participating insurer for a specific assumption through a consent order.

What happens on the assumption date

  • The notice: Citizens' calendar says it mails the Notice of Assumption and Nonrenewal and Certificate of Assumption on the assumption date. The sample notice says, "It is the only notice you will receive." s. 627.4133(2)(b)5. requires Citizens to give written notice of nonrenewal at least 45 days before the nonrenewal takes effect when an authorized insurer has assumed the policy and offers replacement coverage.
  • Your Citizens policy keeps running: the sample notice says the Citizens policy stays in effect until its expiration date, that you keep paying Citizens through the end of the term, and that coverage, premium and policy terms do not change until then. The new policy begins on the expiration date, and you pay the premium the new company bills.
  • Claims: the notice says to report a claim that occurred before 12:01 a.m. on the assumption date to Citizens at (866) 411-2742, and a claim on or after that time to the takeout company. Under s. 627.351(6)(q)5., an assumed policy is direct insurance issued by the insurer, not Citizens, as of the assumption, even if Citizens keeps servicing it.
  • Policy forms: s. 627.351(6)(c)5.a. says a policy removed through an assumption agreement stays on Citizens' policy forms through the end of the policy term. Under s. 627.351(6)(q)6., the insurer may keep using Citizens' forms and endorsements for no more than 3 years without separate OIR approval.
  • Your agent: the sample offer letters say your agent will continue to be your agent even if your policy is assumed. For primary residences, s. 627.351(6)(c)5.a. requires the takeout insurer to offer the agent of record the chance to keep servicing the policy for at least 1 year.
  • Policy papers: the sample letters say you will receive policy information from the new company about 45 days before the new coverage starts.
  • When an assumption fails: the letters say a policy cancelled or nonrenewed before the assumption date will not be assumed. Citizens says that if you accept an offer and your policy is not assumed, you receive a letter saying the policy will remain with Citizens, and you can receive future offers.

If the new premium comes in higher

The sample Notice of Assumption and Nonrenewal says that if the actual cost of the new company's policy is more than 20% higher than Citizens' estimated renewal premium, you may be eligible to return to Citizens, and to contact your agent. Citizens' program guide says such a policy may be rewritten as new business as long as all new business requirements are met. For homes more than 20 years old, Citizens' inspections page says it requires a four-point inspection on property owner, dwelling and mobile home applications; see common 4-point inspection problems and how long inspection reports are valid.

Wind mitigation credits and your offers

The premiums in the packet are estimates. Citizens' sample Offer Forms say the estimated renewal premiums "include all fees and taxes, are based on your existing policy, and are not guaranteed," and that "any changes to coverage, deductibles, wind mitigation credits, policy fees, surcharges, and/or rates will result in a change of premium."

The coverage worksheets on Citizens' Depopulation Resources page compare each takeout company's policy with Citizens'. For example, the One Alliance North America HO-3 worksheet (06/26) lists wind mitigation credits as available and says "Credits are dependent upon wind-resistive features installed." It lists hurricane deductible options of $500, 2%, 5% and 10% of Coverage A. s. 627.0629(1) requires residential property insurance rate filings to include actuarially reasonable discounts or credits for windstorm loss-reducing features, and requires each insurer to describe its hurricane mitigation discounts on its website.

OIR says a completed wind mitigation form (OIR-B1-1802) is valid for up to five years if there are no material changes to the structure and no inaccuracies are found on the form. For what the credits are worth and how the form reaches an insurer, see how much a wind mitigation inspection saves, sending your report to your insurer and hurricane deductibles and your wind mitigation. To compare what Palm Beach County inspectors charge for a wind mitigation, a 4-point or a bundle, see our prices page or the city pages.

What Citizens says to weigh

  • Assessments: Citizens' depopulation page says Citizens policyholders can be required to pay an assessment if Citizens does not have the resources to pay claims after a major hurricane or series of storms, and that private-market policyholders can be subject to a much lower assessment amount. The eligible-policyholder letter says the surcharge "can be as much as 15% of your premium in any single year."
  • Flood insurance: the same letter says most Citizens policies also require a separate flood insurance policy. Citizens' program guide says proof of flood coverage and proof of primary residence are no longer required by the takeout company after an assumption. See Citizens' flood insurance requirement.
  • Coverage differences: the offer letters say the private-market options are not identical to your Citizens policy but offer comparable coverage. Citizens' page says to evaluate all offers carefully and that in many cases the coverage offered may be more comprehensive than Citizens'.
  • The company itself: OIR's take-out companies page lists recently approved companies so policyholders and agents can review their financial status and coverage details. OIR encourages policyholders to talk to their agents, contact the take-out company or visit its website with questions.

Takeouts in Palm Beach County

Citizens' 2026 personal lines tri-county chart shows that for the Oct 20, 2026 assumption, 22,777 of the 37,514 personal lines policies assumed were in Broward, Miami-Dade and Palm Beach counties. The chart groups the three counties together and does not give a Palm Beach County figure on its own. Citizens' 2026 assumption statistics, with data as of Oct 20, 2026, show 168,658 policies assumed across all lines in 2026.

FAQ

What is a Citizens takeout offer?

It is an offer from a private insurer, approved by the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation, to assume your Citizens policy. Citizens calls the program depopulation and mails or emails a Depopulation Packet with each offer's estimated renewal premium and coverage worksheets about 50 days before the assumption.

Can I refuse a Citizens takeout and stay with Citizens?

Only if your packet says you are eligible. For a primary residence, s. 627.351(6)(ii)3. says a takeout offer ends Citizens eligibility unless its premium is more than 20 percent greater than Citizens' renewal premium for comparable coverage. If you are eligible, you must register your choice to remain by the deadline on your Offer Form.

What happens if I ignore the depopulation letter?

Citizens says that if no choice is registered by the date on your Offer Form, it assigns your policy to the private-market company that offered the lowest estimated premium, whether or not you were eligible to stay.

Can I go back to Citizens after a takeout?

Citizens says there is no longer a 30-day period to return after an assumption and that the transfer is final. Its sample notice says you may be eligible to return if the new company's actual cost is more than 20% higher than Citizens' estimated renewal premium, and its program guide says the policy must then meet all new business requirements.

Does my wind mitigation credit carry over to the takeout company?

Citizens' sample Offer Forms say the estimated premiums are based on your existing policy and change with any change to coverage, deductibles or wind mitigation credits. The One Alliance HO-3 coverage worksheet, for example, lists wind mitigation credits as available and dependent on the wind-resistive features installed. Ask your agent how the new company will apply your current form.

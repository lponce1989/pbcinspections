Insurer inspection found problems: repairs, cancellation and nonrenewal

Checked Nov 14, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: insurers hire their own inspectors to check a home's condition before a policy is issued or renewed, and the insurer pays for that inspection. Citizens says it may require you to repair hazards or deficiencies its surveys find, and that it may issue a cancellation or nonrenewal notice if no response comes back. Under Florida Statutes s. 627.4133, a homeowners insurer must give you at least 120 days' written notice, with the reason, before it nonrenews or cancels, with shorter notice for nonpayment and in the first 60 days of a new policy.

This guide covers inspections your insurer orders. For the inspections you order yourself, see 4-point vs. wind mitigation vs. roof certification. For an insurer checking your wind mitigation form, see can your insurer re-inspect your wind mitigation report.

What an underwriting inspection is

Florida's Department of Financial Services (DFS) describes an underwriting inspection as a visual inspection an insurer may require before writing a policy, to verify what the application says about the home and property. DFS lists it as an inspection the insurer pays for. It says insurers hire their own inspectors or inspection firms to check a property's condition before a policy is issued or renewed, and that Florida law does not address who an insurer can hire for this work.

The inspections you pay for are different. DFS says the insured or applicant pays for a 4-point inspection and for specialized inspections such as a roof inspection, and that if the insurer requests one of these and it is not provided, the insurer may refuse to provide certain coverage or refuse to insure the property.

Citizens' version: Citizens Property Insurance calls these underwriting field surveys. It says they are free to the policyholder, are done by a third-party professional field survey company, and may be required for current policyholders during the life of the policy and as a condition for new coverage. Citizens' FAQ adds that inspections it orders to validate features, characteristics and property conditions are paid by Citizens. There are two types:

  • Exterior survey: the field representative does not enter your home but inspects the dwelling, outbuildings and roof.
  • Interior survey: the representative enters to examine the electrical, plumbing and heating and cooling (HVAC) systems, and also does the exterior survey.

What the inspector looks at

DFS says an underwriting inspection may verify the construction of the home and look for potential hazards such as unacceptable animals, pools, trampolines, unrepaired steps and steps without handrails. The insurer may use it to find certain types of wiring or electrical panel boxes it believes increase the risk of fire. It may also check maintenance, such as unrepaired damage, overgrown grass and weeds, trees with dead limbs near the home and non-operating vehicles on the property. DFS notes that this is not an all-inclusive list and that the insurer may refuse coverage if it finds these risks.

Citizens says its surveys help it evaluate the property's replacement value, certain building characteristics and current condition for eligibility requirements, and that they may point out potential issues before they become problems.

Can you refuse or skip it?

Not without consequences, at Citizens. Its FAQ says everyone who signs a Citizens application agrees to allow a property inspection to verify eligibility for coverage or policy discounts. Failing to respond to inspection requests or refusing an inspection may result in removal of wind mitigation credits, if the inspection was meant to validate wind mitigation features, or in cancellation or nonrenewal.

A Citizens agent notice dated Sep 25, 2025 sets out the scheduling rules:

  • Contact: the survey vendor contacts you by text, phone or mail, and depending on the type of survey may set an appointment. At least one person 18 or older must be available to give access to the property.
  • Missed surveys: a policy will be cancelled or nonrenewed if a scheduled survey is missed or if two attempts to schedule it are unsuccessful. An agent may reorder the survey once during the policy term by calling Citizens' Customer Care Center at (888) 685-1555.
  • A second miss: if Citizens rescinds that cancellation or nonrenewal and a new survey is then missed, or two scheduling attempts fail, the policy will be cancelled or nonrenewed and will not be eligible for reinstatement. You may apply for a new Citizens policy, which is subject to underwriting review and the new-business rates in effect when it is bound.

Citizens says it posts agent communications for historical purposes only and does not update them for later changes, so ask your agent whether these rules still apply.

When the inspection finds a problem

Citizens says it may require the property owner to repair hazards or deficiencies. Its Sep 25, 2025 notice says survey reports are filed with the policy, and that if underwriting action is needed, your agent receives an "Inspection Results Response Required" item. If no response is received, Citizens may issue a cancellation or nonrenewal notice. Minor concerns may come to your agent as a "General Correspondence" item to review with you, with no response required.

Roofs: Citizens' FAQ on roof condition says it expects all roofs, both predominant and secondary, to be in acceptable condition when an application is submitted, and that it will take the appropriate adverse action in accordance with Florida Statutes.

New applications: Citizens' Inspections page says serious hazards or deficiencies on a 4-point inspection, such as exposed wiring, active leaks or evidence of leaks, should be repaired before an application is submitted. See common 4-point problems and fixes for the items Citizens' form flags.

Proving the repair

For property damage repairs you do yourself, Citizens' FAQ says to give your agent receipts for materials and photos of the repaired areas. It lists photos of the repairs along with any of these as examples of acceptable documentation:

  • Receipts or invoices showing the services or materials were paid in full
  • An inspection report or permit showing the work was completed
  • A final roof permit, if the roof was replaced

For roof repairs, the same FAQ says underwriting may ask for a Roof Inspection Form completed by a general, residential, building or roofing contractor, a building code inspector, or a Florida-licensed home inspector. See what's on Citizens' Roof Inspection Form (RCF-1) and how to find your permits in Palm Beach County. If you need a new roof or 4-point form, our Palm Beach County prices page lists prices inspectors publish, and the city pages list inspectors by area.

How much notice Florida law requires

Section 627.4133(2) covers personal lines residential property policies, including homeowner, mobile home owner and condominium unit owner policies. It sets these notice rules:

  • Nonrenewal or cancellation: at least 120 days' written notice before the effective date, and the notice must include the reason.
  • Nonpayment of premium: at least 10 days' written notice of cancellation, with the reason.
  • First 60 days of a new policy: for reasons other than nonpayment, at least 20 days' written notice with the reason, unless there has been a material misstatement or misrepresentation or a failure to comply with the insurer's underwriting requirements.
  • After 60 days: the insurer may cancel only for a material misstatement, nonpayment, a failure to comply within 60 days after coverage takes effect with underwriting requirements set before it took effect, a substantial change in the risk, or a cancellation of all insureds in a given class.
  • Home and auto on one policy: it may be nonrenewed for any reason that applies to the property or the vehicle coverage, after 90 days' notice.
  • Citizens takeouts: when a Citizens policy is nonrenewed because an authorized insurer has assumed it and offers replacement coverage, Citizens must give at least 45 days' written notice of nonrenewal.

If an insurer fails to give the required notice, other than the 10-day notice, s. 627.4133(2)(d) keeps your coverage in effect until replacement coverage starts or until a period equal to the required notice has passed after notice is given, whichever comes first. Under s. 627.4133(2)(f), a cancellation or nonrenewal set to take effect during a hurricane is extended until the hurricane ends. DFS's homeowners insurance overview restates the 120-day nonrenewal rule and the limited reasons for canceling after the first 60 days.

Claims and repair requests

Two parts of s. 627.4133 turn on what your insurer asked you to do:

  • Act of God claims: under s. 627.4133(3), claims that result from an act of God may not be used as a cause for cancellation or nonrenewal unless the insurer can show, by claims frequency or otherwise, that you failed to take action reasonably necessary, as requested by the insurer, to prevent the damage from happening again.
  • Water damage: under s. 627.4133(6), a single water damage claim may not be the sole cause for cancellation or nonrenewal unless the insurer can show you failed to take action it reasonably requested to prevent a future similar occurrence.

Cancellation vs. nonrenewal

Citizens' FAQ explains that a nonrenewal happens only when the policy expires, while a cancellation can happen at any time during the policy term. It lists nonpayment, failure to comply with underwriting requirements, material misrepresentation and a substantial change in the risk covered as reasons an insurer may cancel. Citizens' brochure on these notices says that if your policy is cancelled, any refund of prorated, unearned premium is sent within 15 business days after the cancellation takes effect.

If you get a notice

  • Start with your agent: Citizens' FAQs say to contact your agent to discuss your options after a nonrenewal or cancellation notice. You can also call Citizens at (866) 411-2742.
  • Then the company: DFS asks you to contact the insurance company first and give it a chance to resolve the issue, noting that most issues can be resolved within 30 days.
  • Then DFS: DFS lists policy cancellations and nonrenewals among the concerns it can help with, and says companies have 14 days by statute to respond once a concern is filed. You can file through its online Consumer Assistance Portal or call (877) 693-5236.
  • What DFS can't do: DFS says it cannot determine a question of fact or compel a company to reinstate or issue a policy if the company has not violated a law, rule or policy provision.

FAQ

Who pays for an insurer's underwriting inspection?

The insurer. DFS lists the underwriting inspection as one the insurer pays for, and Citizens says its underwriting field surveys are free to the policyholder. You pay for 4-point and specialized inspections such as a roof inspection, according to DFS.

Can I refuse a Citizens property inspection?

Citizens says everyone who signs its application agrees to allow a property inspection. Refusing or not responding may result in removal of wind mitigation credits or in cancellation or nonrenewal.

How much notice does my insurer have to give before nonrenewing my homeowners policy?

At least 120 days' written notice before the effective date, with the reason, under Florida Statutes s. 627.4133(2)(b). A policy covering both a home and a vehicle can be nonrenewed with 90 days' notice.

Can my insurer cancel mid-term because of what its inspector found?

After a policy has been in effect for 60 days, s. 627.4133 allows cancellation only for a material misstatement, nonpayment, failure to comply within 60 days after coverage took effect with underwriting requirements set beforehand, a substantial change in the risk, or cancellation of a whole class of insureds.

What proof of repair does Citizens accept?

For repairs you do yourself, Citizens' FAQ says to give your agent receipts and photos of the repaired areas, and lists paid-in-full receipts or invoices, an inspection report or permit showing the work was completed, and a final roof permit if the roof was replaced as examples. For roof repairs, underwriting may ask for a Roof Inspection Form.

Hurricane upgrades and Florida property taxes: impact windows, shutters and roofs

Checked Nov 21, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: Florida's constitution lets the Legislature, by general law, prohibit counting a change or improvement made to improve a residential property's resistance to wind damage when a home's assessed value is set. The homestead assessment law, s. 193.155(4)(a), still says changes, additions or improvements to homestead property are assessed at just value as of the first January 1 after they are substantially completed, and it lists only two exceptions: rebuilding after a misfortune or calamity, and renewable energy devices under s. 193.624. A 2026 bill to exclude wind upgrades, SB 434, passed the Senate 38 to 0 and died in the House on Mar 13, 2026.

What Florida's constitution allows

Article VII, section 4(i) of the Florida Constitution says the Legislature, "by general law and subject to conditions specified therein, may prohibit the consideration" of two things in setting the assessed value of real property: "Any change or improvement to real property used for residential purposes made to improve the property's resistance to wind damage," and the installation of a solar or renewable energy source device.

The Florida Senate's staff analysis of SB 434 says voters approved this language in the November 2008 general election. Describing current law, the analysis says changes, additions and improvements to real property are assessed at just value as of the first January 1 after they are substantially completed, citing ss. 193.155(4)(a), 193.1554(6)(a) and 193.1555(6)(a).

What the assessment statutes say now

  • Homestead: s. 193.155(4)(a) says "Except as provided in paragraph (b) and s. 193.624, changes, additions, or improvements to homestead property shall be assessed at just value as of the first January 1 after the changes, additions, or improvements are substantially completed." Paragraph (b) covers replacing property damaged or destroyed by misfortune or calamity, and s. 193.624 covers renewable energy source devices.
  • Rentals and other nonhomestead homes: s. 193.1554 applies to residential property with nine or fewer dwelling units that does not have the homestead exemption. Its subsection (6)(a) uses the same rule, with the same two exceptions.
  • Other property: s. 193.1555 covers nonresidential property and residential property not assessed under the other two sections. It defines "improvement" as "an addition or change to land or buildings which increases their value and is more than a repair or a replacement." Sections 193.155 and 193.1554 do not contain that definition.

None of the three sections names impact windows, shutters or roof work.

The 2026 bill that would have changed it

SB 434 (2026), filed by Senators Leek and Gaetz, would have created s. 193.6245. The Senate Rules Committee's analysis says it provided that "any increase in just value attributable to changes or improvements made to improve the property's resistance to wind damage may not be considered" for residential property, and listed these upgrades:

  • Strengthening roof deck attachments.
  • Creating a secondary water barrier to prevent water intrusion.
  • Installing wind-resistant shingles, gable-end bracing, storm shutters and opening protections.
  • Reinforcing roof-to-wall connections.

The analysis says the bill would have applied to improvements made on or after Jan 1, 2027. The Senate's bill page shows it passed the Senate 38 to 0 on Feb 19, 2026, went to the House the same day, and "Died in Messages" on Mar 13, 2026.

Our guides cover the matching questions on the wind mitigation form: roof deck attachment, secondary water resistance, roof-to-wall attachment and opening protection.

How Palm Beach County values the work

  • Market value, not cost: the Palm Beach County Property Appraiser's FAQ says Florida law requires the just value of new improvements, "not to be confused with the construction costs," to be added to the capped assessed value of the land. It says the new value "will NOT be based on permit costs" and reflects what the property would sell for on the open market.
  • How it finds out: the FAQ says the office set up a Permit Portal with local municipalities to see building permits in a timely way, and physically inspects every property at least every five years. s. 193.023(2) says the property appraiser may use image technology instead of a physical inspection where suitable, and must physically inspect a parcel when the owner asks.
  • Roof dates: asked when a roof was last replaced, the FAQ says the best source is the local permit department. Our guide to finding permit records in Palm Beach County shows where to look.
  • Three values: the FAQ explains that just value is market value, assessed value is just value minus caps like the 3% or 10% Save Our Homes limit, and taxable value is assessed value minus exemptions. It adds that the caps apply to assessed value, and that there is no limit on how much market value can rise from year to year.

Rebuilding after hurricane damage

For homestead property, s. 193.155(4)(b) says changes or improvements that replace property "damaged or destroyed by misfortune or calamity" are assessed using the assessed value from the January 1 before the damage, when the rebuilt home is no more than 130% of its old square footage or no more than 2,000 square feet in total. Space beyond those limits is added at just value. The rule applies to work commenced within 5 years after the January 1 following the damage. For nonhomestead residential property, s. 193.1554(6)(b) uses 110% or 1,500 square feet and 3 years.

The Property Appraiser's FAQ answers "Will my property's assessed value increase if I repair or rebuild my home after storm damage?" with "No, as long as the repaired or rebuilt home does not exceed 110% of the structure's original square footage." That figure differs from the 130% and 2,000 square feet in s. 193.155(4)(b) for homesteads, so confirm the limit for your home with the office. The FAQ also says:

  • Tell the office: the owner must notify the Property Appraiser of the intent to repair or rebuild and to use the home as a primary residence when repairs are done.
  • January 1 timing: properties are assessed on their condition as of January 1, so damage repaired before the next January 1 does not change the assessment.
  • Uninhabitable homes: a home that is uninhabitable for more than 30 days may qualify for a partial property tax refund, applied the following year. The office asks for documents such as utility bills, insurance records, contractor statements, building permit applications and certificates of occupancy.

For the insurance side of a storm claim, see claim deadlines and the adjuster's inspection and law and ordinance coverage.

Solar is treated differently

s. 193.624(2)(a) says that for residential property, "the just value of the property attributable to a renewable energy source device may not be considered" in setting assessed value. Its list of devices includes solar energy collectors, photovoltaic modules and inverters.

What Amendment 3 changes in 2027

The Florida Department of Revenue's bulletin PTO 26-21 (Nov 5, 2026) says voters approved Amendment 3 in the Nov 3, 2026 general election, and that it takes effect Jan 1, 2027 and first applies to the 2027 tax roll. The bulletin says the amendment:

  • Lowers the annual cap on assessment increases for non-homestead properties from 10% to 5%.
  • Raises the homestead exemption for all non-school taxes to up to $150,000 of assessed value in 2027 and up to $250,000 in 2028, adjusted for inflation after that.
  • Gives people who are not Florida residents as of Dec 31, 2026 a $25,000 school-tax exemption and up to $50,000 for non-school taxes, with the larger exemption starting in the fifth year of exemption.

The bulletin's summary does not mention wind-resistance improvements.

Where the upgrades do pay: insurance discounts

s. 627.0629(1) says rate filings for residential property insurance must include "actuarially reasonable discounts, credits, or other rate differentials, or appropriate reductions in deductibles" for properties with fixtures or construction techniques shown to reduce windstorm losses. It names wind uplift prevention, roof strength, roof covering performance, roof-to-wall strength, wall-to-floor-to-foundation strength, opening protection, and window, door and skylight strength. The same section says each insurer must describe its hurricane mitigation discounts on its website.

s. 627.711(2)(a) says the uniform mitigation verification inspection form (the OIR-B1-1802 wind mitigation form) "shall be used by all insurers when submitted by policyholders for the purpose of factoring discounts for wind insurance." Our guide to how much a wind mitigation inspection saves walks through OIR's discount table, and the My Safe Florida Home grant guide covers the state program that helps pay for some of these upgrades. If you disagree with your assessed value

The Property Appraiser's FAQ says owners who disagree with an assessment can first contact the office for an informal review, at (561) 355-3230. If that does not resolve it, the FAQ says you can file a petition with the Value Adjustment Board, which is run by the Palm Beach County Clerk & Comptroller's Office and is independent of the Property Appraiser.

FAQ

Do impact windows raise property taxes in Florida?

They can be counted. Section 193.155(4)(a) says changes, additions or improvements to homestead property are assessed at just value as of the first January 1 after they are substantially completed, and its only exceptions are rebuilding after a calamity and renewable energy devices. The constitution allows the Legislature to exclude wind-resistance improvements, but the 2026 bill to do that, SB 434, died in the House.

Does the 3% Save Our Homes cap limit the increase from an upgrade?

Not for the improvement itself. Section 193.155(4)(a) assesses changes, additions or improvements at just value, and the Palm Beach County Property Appraiser's FAQ says the just value of new improvements is added to the capped assessed value of the land.

Will rebuilding my roof or home after a hurricane raise my assessment?

For homesteads, s. 193.155(4)(b) keeps the prior assessed value when the rebuilt home stays within 130% of its old square footage or 2,000 square feet, if work starts within 5 years. The Palm Beach County Property Appraiser's FAQ uses 110% of the original square footage and says you must notify the office of your intent to repair or rebuild, so confirm the limit with the office.

Are solar panels treated the same way?

No. Section 193.624(2)(a) says the just value attributable to a renewable energy source device, including solar collectors, photovoltaic modules and inverters, may not be considered in the assessed value of residential property.

Do hurricane upgrades lower my insurance instead?

Section 627.0629(1) requires residential property insurance rate filings to include actuarially reasonable discounts or credits for fixtures and construction techniques shown to reduce windstorm loss, including roof strength, roof-to-wall strength and opening protection. Section 627.711(2)(a) says insurers must use the uniform mitigation verification form when a policyholder submits it for wind insurance discounts.

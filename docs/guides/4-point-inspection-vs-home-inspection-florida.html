4-point inspection vs. a full home inspection in Florida

Checked Nov 9, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: Florida law defines a home inspection as a limited visual examination of a home's structure, electrical, HVAC, roof covering, plumbing, interior and exterior components, and the site conditions that affect the structure, written up as a professional opinion of the home's condition. Citizens' 4-point covers four of those systems, the roof, electrical, plumbing and HVAC, and Citizens says the information is used only to determine insurability. A Florida-licensed home inspector is one of the professionals Citizens lists as able to sign its 4-point form.

What Florida law calls a home inspection

Section 468.8311 defines home inspection services as "a limited visual examination of the following readily accessible installed systems and components of a home: the structure, electrical system, HVAC system, roof covering, plumbing system, interior components, exterior components, and site conditions that affect the structure, for the purposes of providing a written professional opinion of the condition of the home."

The same section defines a home as residential real property, or a manufactured or modular home, that is a single-family dwelling, duplex, triplex, quadruplex, condominium unit or cooperative unit. It does not include the common areas of condominiums or cooperatives.

Under s. 468.8323, the written report must cover systems and components that, in the inspector's professional opinion, are significantly deficient or near the end of their service lives, with a reason if that is not self-evident. It must also list any systems and components that were present but not inspected, and why. The inspector is not required to estimate the cost of repairs.

Before contracting for or starting the inspection, s. 468.8321 says the home inspector must give you a copy of their Florida license and a written disclosure of the inspection's scope and any exclusions.

What a 4-point inspection covers

Citizens' sample 4-Point Inspection Form (Insp4pt 03 25) has four sections: electrical, HVAC, plumbing and roof. It says underwriting relies on the information "only" to "determine insurability" and that it "is not a warranty or assurance of the suitability, fitness or longevity of any of the systems inspected."

The form's minimum photos are each side of the dwelling, each roof slope, the water heater with its temperature and pressure relief valve, under-cabinet plumbing and drains, exposed valves, the main electrical panel with its interior door label, the electrical box with the panel off, and every hazard or deficiency in the report. The HVAC section asks for photos of the equipment, including the dated manufacturer's plate.

Its special instructions say "this specific form is not required," but any other report submitted "must include at least this level of detail to be acceptable." Citizens' FAQ lists what an alternate form needs: an inspection by a verifiable Florida-licensed professional, the inspection date, the inspector's signature, title, Florida license information and company, all of the photos above, and the age, type and condition of the electrical wiring, heating and plumbing systems. Citizens says it urges inspectors to use its own 4-point form. Our guide to common 4-point problems goes through what each section flags.

Who can do each

Home inspection. Section 468.8319 says a person may not practice or offer to practice home inspection services, or use titles such as "home inspector," "licensed home inspector" or "certified home inspector," without complying with Florida's home inspector law. Under s. 468.8313, an applicant must pass the required exam, be of good moral character and complete a course of at least 120 hours covering the structure, electrical, HVAC, roof covering, plumbing, interior and exterior components, and site conditions. Section 468.8322 requires commercial general liability insurance of at least $300,000.

To renew, s. 468.8316 requires at least 14 hours of continuing education every two years, including at least 2 hours of hurricane mitigation training that covers the uniform mitigation verification inspection form. Section 468.8318 says a corporation cannot hold a home inspection license, and everyone who acts as a home inspector for a company must be licensed.

4-point inspection. Citizens' FAQ says the form must be completed and signed by a verifiable Florida-licensed inspector, and gives as examples a general, residential or building contractor, a building code inspector and a home inspector. It adds that a registered architect, professional engineer or building code official whose Florida license can be verified will be accepted. The sample form notes that a trade-specific licensed professional may sign only the section for their trade, such as an electrician signing only the electrical section. You can check any of these licenses yourself; see how to look up a license on DBPR.

Rules a home inspector must follow

Section 468.8319 also says a home inspector may not:

  • Perform or offer to perform repairs on a home that the inspector or the inspector's company has prepared a home inspection report on. This does not apply to a home warranty company that is affiliated with or retains a home inspector to make repairs under a home warranty claim.
  • Inspect a property in which the inspector or the inspector's company has any financial or transfer interest.
  • Offer or deliver any compensation, inducement or reward to a broker or agent for referring the owner of the inspected property.
  • Accept an engagement in which the inspection or its fee depends on the report's conclusions, preestablished findings or the close of escrow.

The statute makes a violation of any of these a first-degree misdemeanor.

When Citizens asks for a 4-point

Citizens says a four-point inspection is mandatory on any personal residential multiperil application for a home more than 20 years old, and that the report must be dated within the 12 months before the new-business application submission date. Shorter windows apply for lender-placed coverage and for homes with a prior sinkhole claim. Citizens requires the 4-point on new business only, unless underwriting decides on a case-by-case basis that one is needed at renewal. It says 4-point inspections do not apply to tenant named insured and condominium risks.

The form's roof section, with photos of each roof slope, can take the place of Citizens' Roof Inspection Form (RCF-1) if it is signed and dated by a verifiable Florida-licensed professional. See how long each report stays valid.

Can one inspector do both?

A Florida-licensed home inspector is on Citizens' list of professionals who can sign its 4-point form. Whether a home inspection report could stand in for the 4-point depends on Citizens' alternate form rules: it would need the inspector's signature, title, license information and company, the inspection date, every required photo, and the age, type and condition of the electrical wiring, heating and plumbing systems. Citizens says it urges inspectors to use its own 4-point form, so ask your agent which report your insurer will take.

Before you book

  • Ask for the license and scope: for a home inspection, s. 468.8321 entitles you to a copy of the inspector's license and a written scope with any exclusions before work starts.
  • Check the license: look up the inspector on DBPR before you pay; our DBPR guide shows how.
  • Say which form you need: if your insurer is Citizens, ask for its 4-Point Inspection Form with all of its required photos.

FAQ

Is a 4-point inspection the same as a home inspection?

No. Florida law defines a home inspection as a limited visual examination of the structure, electrical, HVAC, roof covering, plumbing, interior and exterior components, and site conditions that affect the structure. Citizens' 4-point covers the roof, electrical, plumbing and HVAC, and Citizens says it is used only to determine insurability.

Can a home inspector do a 4-point inspection?

Yes. Citizens lists a Florida-licensed home inspector among the professionals who can complete and sign its 4-point form, along with general, residential and building contractors and building code inspectors.

Can my home inspection report replace the 4-point?

Citizens accepts an alternate form only if it is done by a verifiable Florida-licensed professional, shows the date, signature, title, license information and company, includes all of the 4-point photo requirements, and gives the age, type and condition of the electrical wiring, heating and plumbing systems. Citizens urges inspectors to use its own form.

Does a Florida home inspector need a license?

Yes. Section 468.8319 says a person may not practice or offer home inspection services, or call themselves a home inspector, without complying with Florida's home inspector law, which requires a 120-hour course, an exam and at least $300,000 in general liability insurance.

Can a home inspector fix the problems they find?

No. Section 468.8319 says a home inspector may not perform or offer to perform repairs on a home that the inspector or their company prepared a home inspection report on, except for a home warranty company handling a warranty claim.

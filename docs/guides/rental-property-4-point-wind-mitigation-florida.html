Rental homes: 4-point, roof and wind mitigation rules for landlords

Checked Nov 11, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: Citizens Property Insurance says tenant-occupied homes are in most cases not eligible for a Homeowners policy, and it offers Dwelling Fire policies (DP-3 and DP-1) for tenant-occupied properties. Citizens requires a four-point inspection for dwelling applications on properties more than 20 years old, and its DP-3 coverage worksheet lists wind mitigation credits as available.

Which Citizens policy a rental goes on

Citizens says its Homeowners (HO-3) policy is available for detached single-family homes and duplexes in which at least one unit is owner-occupied, and that the owner must live in the home as their primary residence. Asked whether a home rented to a tenant is eligible for a Homeowners policy, Citizens answers that "in most cases tenant occupied homes are not eligible" and tells you to contact your agent to decide between a Homeowners and a Dwelling Fire policy. Citizens' answer on policy types adds: "If you own rental property, coverage is typically provided through a Dwelling Form policy."

Citizens describes its Dwelling Fire (DP-3) policy as available for tenant-occupied properties and properties that otherwise may not qualify for an HO-3 or HO-8. It covers the dwelling, other structures on the property, personal property, and loss of rent or additional living expenses. Citizens says the DP-1 is similar but covers only certain named perils. For tenant-occupied mobile homes, Citizens offers a Mobile Home Dwelling Fire (MDP-1) policy; see mobile home inspections and credits.

Your tenant's renters policy is a different product. Citizens says its Tenant/Renters Contents (HO-4) policy covers the renter's personal property, additional living expenses and liability, and "does not provide coverage for the structure itself."

Florida law files all of these under one heading. Section 627.4025(1) says personal lines residential coverage consists of the type of coverage provided by homeowner, mobile home owner, dwelling, tenant, condominium unit owner, cooperative unit owner and similar policies.

The 4-point on a rental

Citizens says it "requires a four-point inspection for all property owner, dwelling and mobile home applications for properties more than 20 years old." The inspector reports the age, type and condition of the electrical, plumbing, heating and cooling systems and the roof, and Citizens says serious hazards or deficiencies, such as exposed wiring or active leaks, should be repaired before an application is submitted. See what the 4-point form flags.

Citizens says four-point inspections are required on new-business submissions only, unless Underwriting decides case by case that one is needed for a renewal. The report must be dated within the 12 months before the new-business application. Citizens sets shorter windows for policyholders with lender-placed coverage in effect within 45 days of the effective date (10 days) and for risks with a prior sinkhole claim (90 days). More in how long inspection reports are valid.

Both of those Citizens answers end with the same note: "Four-point inspections are not applicable to tenant named insured and condominium risks." Citizens' inspections page, quoted above, separately names dwelling applications among those that need a four-point on homes more than 20 years old.

Roof age rules for rentals

Citizens' answer on roof age for personal residential policies says documentation of a full roof replacement is required for homes with tile, slate, clay, concrete or metal roofs more than 50 years old and for homes with shingle or other roofs more than 25 years old. It says some exceptions apply and that you can submit documentation showing at least five years of remaining useful life for underwriters to review. Citizens lists its Dwelling Fire policies on its Personal Residential policies page.

Citizens says its four-point form can be submitted instead of the Roof Inspection Form (RCF-1) if photos of each side of the roof are provided and the form is signed and dated by a verifiable Florida-licensed professional. See the RCF-1 roof form.

The state roof-age protections in s. 627.7011(5), Florida Statutes, are written for homeowner's policies. Under paragraph (b), an insurer may not refuse to issue or renew a homeowner's policy on a home with a roof less than 15 years old solely because of the roof's age. Paragraph (c) says that for a roof at least 15 years old, the insurer must allow an inspection by an authorized inspector before requiring replacement, and may not refuse to issue or renew solely because of roof age if the inspection shows at least five years of useful life left. Subsection (6)(a) says the section does not apply to policies not considered "homeowners' policies," as that term is commonly understood in the insurance industry. The statute does not mention dwelling fire policies by name. More in Florida's roof age rules.

Wind mitigation credits on a dwelling policy

Section 627.0629(1) says a rate filing for residential property insurance must include actuarially reasonable discounts, credits or other rate differentials, or appropriate reductions in deductibles, for properties with fixtures or construction techniques demonstrated to reduce windstorm losses. The same subsection requires each insurer subject to it to describe its hurricane mitigation discounts on its website.

Citizens' DP-3 coverage worksheet (10/25) lists wind mitigation credits as "Available" and says credits are dependent upon the wind-resistive features installed. The same worksheet lists hurricane deductible options of $500, 2%, 5% and 10% of Coverage A. See how hurricane deductibles work.

Citizens says the Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form (OIR-B1-1802) is for single-family dwellings, "one-to-four-unit residential dwellings, including apartments and condominiums," and residential buildings of one to three stories, including those with five or more units. Its Wind Loss Mitigation Guide for the Rev. 04/26 form gives this checklist before credits are requested:

  • The form is signed by a qualified inspector and by the customer.
  • The form is valid (within five years) and there have been no structural changes since the inspection.
  • The required photos for attributes 2 through 9 are complete.
  • The Owner Information field is completed, and the address on the form matches the property address on the policy.

The guide also says that if the property is a new purchase, the form may be in the name of and signed by the prior owner. If you bought the rental with a report already done, see using a seller's wind mitigation report. For how the form reaches your insurer, see sending your report to your insurer.

Opening protection on higher-value homes

Citizens' answer to "When are opening protections required?" lists homes that "are valued at $750,000 or more" and that "are located in the Wind Borne Debris Region (WBDR)." It says a qualified inspector must complete and submit the OIR-B1-1802 (Rev. 04/26) for the home to be eligible for coverage, and notes that this applies only to personal lines policies. Palm Beach County's wind speed page says all of unincorporated Palm Beach County is within the wind-borne debris region. See how the form rates windows and shutters and the county's wind speed regions.

Short-term and vacation rentals

Citizens says a property is not eligible for its Personal Residential Multiperil coverage if it is rented to guests more than three times in a calendar year for periods of less than 30 days or one calendar month, whichever is less, or if it is advertised or held out to the public as a place regularly rented to guests. If such a property is in a wind-only eligible area, Citizens says it may be eligible for a Personal Residential Wind-Only policy. Citizens' wind-only policy list includes a Dwelling (DW-2) form.

Citizens says your Citizens agent can tell you free of charge whether a property is in a wind-only eligible area if you provide the address, city, ZIP code and year built.

When a tenant lives there on inspection day

Citizens says that if you cannot attend your wind mitigation inspection, you may designate someone 18 or older as your representative, and that this person should know where to find the attic access and any stored storm shutters or panels. Citizens says attic access is required to verify certain features, and if the inspector cannot get into the attic you may not be eligible for discounts for roof-to-wall attachment, roof-deck attachment and secondary water resistance.

Citizens also says to give the inspector copies of any mitigation documents before the inspection begins. Its examples are receipts or other documents with product specifications, work orders and invoices, roofing permits, and notice-of-acceptance letters for mitigation products. More in how to prepare for a wind mitigation inspection.

Before you book

  • Confirm the policy type with your agent: Citizens says the agent recommends a policy type based on the type and occupancy of the structure.
  • Check the license: Citizens says a form may not be accepted if the appropriate professional did not perform the inspection or the professional's Florida license is not active or cannot be verified. See how to look up a license on DBPR.
  • Compare bundle prices: 8 of the 16 companies we list publish a 4-point + wind mitigation bundle price. Listed bundle prices run from $200 to $300, some of them starting prices, with a median of $237.50 counting each company once. FAQ

Can I get a Citizens Homeowners policy on a home I rent out?

Citizens says that in most cases tenant-occupied homes are not eligible for a Homeowners policy. Its HO-3 is available for detached single-family homes and for duplexes in which at least one unit is owner-occupied, and the owner must live in the home as their primary residence.

What Citizens policy covers a rental home?

Citizens says its Dwelling Fire (DP-3) policy is available for tenant-occupied properties and covers the dwelling, other structures, personal property, and loss of rent or additional living expenses. The DP-1 is similar but covers only certain named perils.

Does a rental property need a 4-point inspection?

Citizens says it requires a four-point inspection for all property owner, dwelling and mobile home applications for properties more than 20 years old. It is required on new-business submissions, and at renewal only if Underwriting decides one is needed.

Can I get wind mitigation discounts on a rental property?

Citizens' DP-3 coverage worksheet lists wind mitigation credits as available and says they depend on the wind-resistive features installed. Section 627.0629 requires rate filings for residential property insurance to include discounts for features shown to reduce windstorm losses.

Can my tenant let the inspector in?

Citizens says that if you cannot attend your wind mitigation inspection, you may designate someone 18 or older as your representative, and that this person should know where the attic access and any stored storm shutters or panels are.

Does Citizens insure short-term rentals?

Citizens says a property rented to guests more than three times a calendar year for stays under 30 days or one calendar month, whichever is less, or advertised as regularly rented to guests, is not eligible for its Personal Residential Multiperil coverage. In a wind-only eligible area it may be eligible for a Personal Residential Wind-Only policy.

Law and ordinance coverage: paying for code upgrades after a roof or hurricane claim

Checked Nov 19, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: under Florida Statutes s. 627.7011, before issuing a homeowner's policy an insurer must offer replacement cost coverage that also pays the costs necessary to meet applicable laws and ordinances regulating the construction, use or repair of property, including the cost of removing debris. That extra coverage may be limited to 25 percent or 50 percent of the dwelling limit, as the policyholder selects. Unless you reject it in writing, your policy is deemed to include law and ordinance coverage of 25 percent of the dwelling limit. The Department of Financial Services (DFS) calls it Ordinance and Law Coverage and says it helps cover the cost to rebuild your home in compliance with the current building code.

This guide covers what the law requires, how the coverage is paid, Citizens Property Insurance's version of it, and the building code items a roof replacement can bring in. For claim deadlines and the adjuster's visit, see roof or hurricane damage claims in Florida.

What Florida law requires

Section 627.7011 sets these rules for homeowners' policies:

  • The offer: before issuing a homeowner's policy, the insurer must offer a policy or endorsement that adjusts losses at replacement cost and also includes the costs necessary to meet applicable laws and ordinances. If the policy already includes law and ordinance coverage at 25 percent of the dwelling limit, the insurer must still offer the 50 percent option.
  • The default: unless the insurer gets your written refusal, any policy covering the dwelling is deemed to include law and ordinance coverage limited to 25 percent of the dwelling limit. The rejection, or the selection of different coverage, must be made on a form approved by the Office of Insurance Regulation (OIR), and a form signed by one named insured is conclusively presumed to be an informed rejection on behalf of all insureds.
  • Renewals: if you rejected the coverage or chose an alternative, the insurer does not have to add it at renewal unless you ask in writing. It must send you notice that the coverage is available, on an OIR-approved form, at least once every 3 years.
  • The warning: with the policy documents at issuance and at every renewal, the insurer must print, in bold type no smaller than 18 points: "LAW AND ORDINANCE: LAW AND ORDINANCE COVERAGE IS AN IMPORTANT COVERAGE THAT YOU MAY WISH TO PURCHASE. PLEASE DISCUSS WITH YOUR INSURANCE AGENT."
  • Damaged portion only: the coverage applies only to repairs of the damaged portion of the structure, unless the total damage exceeds 50 percent of the structure's replacement cost.
  • Exceptions: the section does not apply to mobile home policies, or to policies not considered homeowners' policies as that term is commonly understood in the insurance industry.

DFS says the additional Ordinance and Law Coverage may increase your insurance premium. Its checklist of questions to ask your agent when shopping includes whether the policy covers repairing your home to the current building code, and how roof repair or replacement cost is determined, including whether the policy has a fee schedule for the roof.

How it is paid after a claim

For a dwelling insured at replacement cost, s. 627.7011(3)(a) says the insurer must first pay at least the actual cash value of the loss, less any deductible, and then pay the remaining amounts needed for the repairs as work is performed and expenses are incurred. A total loss is the exception: the statute says the insurer must then pay the replacement cost coverage without holding back depreciation.

Citizens says its policy may provide Ordinance or Law coverage for upgrades necessary to meet building and municipal codes, that any Ordinance or Law payments are made on an incurred basis, and that you should contact your agent or claims adjuster for details, or Citizens at (866) 411-2742. If you are unsure what your policy pays, DFS lists the Office of the Insurance Consumer Advocate at (850) 413-5923 and its Insurance Consumer Helpline at (877) 693-5236 for free guidance.

Citizens' Ordinance or Law options

  • HO-3: Citizens' HO-3 Coverage Worksheet (02/23) lists Ordinance or Law at 25% of Coverage A, with a 50% limit available.
  • When you can change it: Citizens says Ordinance or Law can be increased to 50 percent of Coverage A on new policies and at renewal, that midterm changes are not allowed, and that the coverage does not apply to tenant contents, mobile home or commercial lines policies.
  • HO-8: Citizens says that if you choose actual cash value loss settlement on its HO-8 form, Ordinance or Law coverage is automatically excluded.
  • Increased limits: Citizens lists Increased Limits for Ordinance or Law among its optional coverages, for additional expenses when changes in the law or building codes raise the cost of rebuilding beyond your regular Ordinance or Law limits.
  • Comparing quotes: Citizens' agent guide says that for a private-market offer to count as comparable, the ordinance and law coverage percentage must match, if offered, along with Coverage A limits, hurricane deductibles, mitigation credits and loss settlement. See Citizens eligibility and the 20% rule.

Code upgrades a reroof can trigger

Florida Statutes s. 553.844 directs the Florida Building Commission to require these items when a site-built, single-family home's roof is replaced:

  • Every roof replacement: secondary water barriers for the roof, and strengthening or correcting roof-decking attachments and fasteners.
  • Roof-to-wall connections: for a building in the wind-borne debris region with an insured value of $300,000 or more (or, if uninsured or no insured value is shown, a just valuation for the structure of $300,000 or more). The statute says these connections are not required unless the gable ends or all corners can be evaluated and connected for 15 percent of the cost of the roof replacement.
  • Opening protection: for permitted work other than roof covering replacement or repair, applied for on or after July 1, 2008, with an estimated cost of $50,000 or more, on a building in the wind-borne debris region with an insured value (or just valuation) of $750,000 or more.
  • Newer roofs: if the roof or roof section was built, repaired or replaced under the 2007 Florida Building Code or a later edition, and 25 percent or more of it is being repaired, replaced or recovered, only that portion has to meet the current code.

Palm Beach County's wind speed page says all of unincorporated Palm Beach County is within the wind-borne debris region; s. 553.844 ties its rules to the region as defined in s. 1609.2 of the 2006 International Building Code. Citizens says to ask your agent or claims adjuster about Ordinance or Law coverage for code upgrades, so ask which of these items your claim will pay for before work starts. Our guide to Florida's reroof rules covers each requirement in more detail.

After the repairs: update your wind mitigation

Citizens says wind mitigation inspection forms usually are valid for up to five years, but that you may need a new one sooner if you make improvements that may qualify for additional discounts. Its examples of changes that may require a new inspection include replacing a roof, adding new wind-damage mitigation features, and insurance claims for certain types of damage to your home.

The reroof items above line up with questions on the wind mitigation form: see secondary water resistance, roof deck attachment and roof-to-wall attachment. Our Palm Beach County prices page lists the wind mitigation prices inspectors publish, and the city pages list inspectors by area.

FAQ

Is law and ordinance coverage required in Florida?

Section 627.7011 requires insurers to offer it before issuing a homeowner's policy. Unless you reject it in writing on an OIR-approved form, the policy is deemed to include law and ordinance coverage limited to 25 percent of the dwelling limit.

What does law and ordinance coverage pay for?

The statute describes costs necessary to meet applicable laws and ordinances regulating the construction, use or repair of property, or requiring the tearing down of property, including debris removal. DFS says it helps cover the cost to rebuild your home in compliance with the current building code.

How much law and ordinance coverage can I get?

The statute allows 25 percent or 50 percent of the dwelling limit, as you select. DFS gives the example of $200,000 in dwelling coverage: at least $50,000 for code compliance, and an offer of at least $100,000.

Can I raise my Citizens Ordinance or Law coverage to 50%?

Citizens says Ordinance or Law can be increased to 50 percent of Coverage A on new policies and at renewal, and that midterm changes are not allowed. It does not apply to tenant contents, mobile home or commercial lines policies.

Does law and ordinance coverage apply to the whole house?

Under s. 627.7011, it applies only to repairs of the damaged portion of the structure, unless the total damage exceeds 50 percent of the structure's replacement cost.

My Safe Florida Condo Pilot Program: free inspections and grants for associations

Checked Nov 16, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: the My Safe Florida Condominium Pilot Program, created by Florida Statutes s. 215.55871 and run by the Department of Financial Services (DFS), offers a free hurricane mitigation inspection and reimbursement grants to condominium associations, not to individual unit owners. The building must be three stories or taller, have at least two residential units, be within 15 miles of the coast, and not consist of detached units on separate parcels. The state pays $2 for every $1 the association spends, up to $175,000 per association, and only after the work is done, inspected and credited by the insurer.

This guide covers the state program for condo associations. For the wind mitigation forms a condo building uses outside the program, see condo wind mitigation and 4-point inspections in Florida. For the separate program for single-family homes, see My Safe Florida Home grant: who qualifies and the steps to payment.

Which condo buildings qualify

DFS lists four requirements for a condo building:

  • Height: three stories or taller. The statute limits participation to structures or buildings on the condominium property that are three or more stories in height.
  • Units: at least two residential units per building. The statute says each building that is the subject of a grant must contain at least two single-family dwellings.
  • Location: within 15 miles of the coast. The statute defines the service area as the area of the state 15 miles inward of a coastline, as defined in s. 376.031. DFS says you don't have to know whether your condo is in the service area to apply, because program staff confirm it during the eligibility review.
  • Not detached: the building must not consist of detached units on separate parcels. The statute's definition of condominium excludes detached units on individual parcels of land.

The regular My Safe Florida Home program, under Florida Statutes s. 215.5586, is for a single-family unit on an individual parcel of land, either detached or attached and not exceeding three stories, that is site-built and owner-occupied.

The association applies, not the unit owners

Section 215.55871 says a unit owner may take part only through a grant awarded to the association and may not take part individually. DFS says the program requires associations to apply on behalf of all unit owners, and its flyer tells individual owners to contact their association. These are the conditions before the association can apply:

  • Board or owner vote for the inspection: a majority vote of the board of administration, or a majority vote of the total voting interests of the association, to participate.
  • Milestone and reserve study rules: the association may not apply for an inspection or a grant unless it has complied with the inspection requirements in s. 553.899 (mandatory structural inspections for condominium and cooperative buildings) and s. 718.112(2)(g) and (h) (the structural integrity reserve study and milestone inspections).
  • Documents for the inspection application: DFS says the board president or treasurer provides the association's name and address, the authorized representative, property details, the condominium insurance declarations page and the board's voting results. The flyer lists the board meeting minutes and the insurance declarations page as the required documents.
  • One application per property: the board president must state, under penalty of perjury, that the association has submitted only a single application for each property it operates or maintains.

The votes may take place at the association's annual budget meeting or at a unit owner meeting called for that purpose. Before unit owners vote, the association must give them a disclosure of the program on a DFS form, Form DFS-O1-012, signed by the board president and treasurer, and within 14 days after an affirmative vote it must give written notice of the decision to all unit owners.

The free initial inspection

DFS says that once the association is approved, a licensed inspector contracted by the program is assigned, and the association cannot use its own inspector. Under the statute, the program's inspectors must be licensed or certified as a building inspector, a general, building or residential contractor, a professional engineer, a professional architect, or a home inspector who has completed at least 3 hours of approved hurricane mitigation training. They must also undergo drug testing and a background screening.

The statute requires the inspection report to identify recommended improvements, give a range of cost estimates for them, and give estimated insurance premium discounts for the current features and for the recommended improvements. DFS's Condominium Association Guide (Form DFS-O1-009) says the report also includes inspection photos and notes, the OIR-B1-1802 wind mitigation form for condo buildings up to three stories, and the Building Type II and III Mitigation Inspection Form for buildings of four stories and above.

  • Inspection only: an association may apply for and receive an inspection without also applying for a grant.
  • Already compliant: if the report shows the building already qualifies for a premium reduction, DFS says to send the report to your insurance agent and ask for a review of the policy for available credits.
  • Missed appointments: the Condominium Association Guide says the application is closed if the association declines the inspection, refuses access, fails to schedule or appear, or doesn't respond after three scheduling attempts.

To see what each feature on those forms means, see the wind mitigation form, question by question.

What the grant pays for

The statute lists these improvements, when the inspection report recommends them:

  • Opening protection: exterior doors, garage doors, windows and skylights.
  • Roof: reinforcing roof-to-wall connections, improving the strength of roof-deck attachments, installing secondary water resistance, and replacing the roof covering.

The limits that come with it:

  • Must earn a credit: grant funds may be used only for water intrusion mitigation devices or improvements that will result in a mitigation credit, discount or other rate differential for the building. If it is needed for the building to qualify for a credit, DFS must require improvements to all openings, including exterior doors, garage doors, windows and skylights.
  • Only what the report recommends: DFS says only projects recommended in your inspection report are eligible for a grant.
  • Windows: DFS says you must replace all windows recommended for improvement to qualify for an insurance discount, that you don't need to replace windows that are already protected, and that you won't be reimbursed for previously protected windows replaced under the program. The Condominium Association Guide says a request to swap compliant hurricane shutters for impact windows will be denied.
  • Windows as common elements: an association may not apply for an opening protection grant for association or condominium property unless the declaration establishes the windows as common elements.
  • Upgrades only: if improvements that met the code in effect at the time were already installed, the grant must go to improvements that meet or exceed the code in effect when the association applies and give more hurricane protection. The grant may not be used to install the same type of improvement again.
  • Roofs: the Condominium Association Guide says not to upgrade the roof covering without also adding secondary water resistance, that all portions of a contiguous roof must be replaced with it, and that program funds may not be used for roof patching or other partial repairs.
  • No deductibles: the grant may not be used to pay a deductible for a pending insurance claim on the property.

How much the state pays

  • Match: all grants are matched on the basis of $1 from the association for $2 from the state toward the actual cost of the project.
  • Cap: an association may get funds for both roof and opening protection projects, but the total grant may not exceed $175,000 per association.
  • Reimbursement only: DFS says the program does not provide funds in advance. The association pays for the improvements, and its Disclosure Notice says the association must pay for the project upfront.
  • No work before approval: DFS says construction must not begin until the grant application is approved, and work done before approval won't qualify for grant funding.
  • Funding: the program is subject to annual legislative appropriations and does not create an entitlement. DFS may not accept grant applications or keep a waiting list once the grants awarded have fully obligated the appropriation. DFS describes the program as a pilot whose future funding depends on participant feedback and results.

Steps from application to payment

DFS's flyer and website describe these stages:

  1. Inspection application: the board completes the inspection application with the required documents. DFS's website says the program now processes all inspection and grant applications by email, at condopilot@myfloridacfo.com. Its Condominium Association Guide still describes an online portal.
  2. Initial inspection: after approval, the association gets an approval letter by email, and the inspection company contacts it to set a date and time.
  3. Grant application: after the report, the association applies for the grant. The flyer lists a letter from the insurer confirming a potential discount, a notarized statement of intended contractor, the contractor's cost estimate, the unit owner voting results, the signed Disclosure Notice, and the condo declarations if opening protection is being improved. The Intended Contractors List and Commitment to Complete is Form DFS-O1-013 on DFS's website.
  4. The work: the association chooses its own contractors, who must be properly licensed and secure all required local permits, and DFS verifies each contractor's state license number before approving a grant. Construction must be finished and a final inspection requested, or an extension requested, within 1 year after grant approval, or the application is deemed abandoned.
  5. Final inspection: the association submits the contractor's itemized invoice, proof of full payment and closed permits. The flyer says there is only one free final inspection, so don't request it until all work is complete and the permits are closed.
  6. Reimbursement: the association sends the final inspection report to its insurer, asks for written confirmation of the mitigation discount or credit, and sends that, with the reimbursement application and the address for the check, to the program. DFS says associations must receive a mitigation credit, discount or other rate differential to receive grant funding.
  • Missing information: if DFS asks for more information and doesn't get a response within 60 days, the application is deemed withdrawn.
  • Appeals: DFS says associations may appeal a program decision within 21 days of the notice, and that appeal instructions come with the email notice.
  • Contractor disputes: DFS says the program takes no responsibility for the contractor's performance and is not involved in contractor selection, construction or payment.

The 75% unit owner vote

For a grant that improves one or more units, s. 215.55871 requires both a majority vote of the board or of the total voting interests, and approval by at least 75 percent of all unit owners who reside within the building that is the subject of the grant. If the grant will improve units, the application must also include an acknowledged statement from each unit owner whose approval is required. DFS's flyer words the threshold as at least 75% of unit owners in the condominium association, and its Disclosure Notice as a 75% vote from all unit owners affected by the project.

If you own a unit and want the inspection, DFS's contact for the program is condopilot@myfloridacfo.com or (850) 413-2971. For a private wind mitigation inspection outside the program, our Palm Beach County prices page lists the prices inspectors publish, and the city pages list inspectors by area.

FAQ

Can a condo unit owner apply to My Safe Florida Condo on their own?

No. Under Florida Statutes s. 215.55871, a unit owner may take part only through a grant awarded to the association. DFS says the association applies on behalf of all unit owners.

Which condo buildings qualify for the pilot program?

DFS says the building must be three stories or taller, have at least two residential units, be within 15 miles of the coast, and not consist of detached units on separate parcels.

How much is the My Safe Florida Condo grant?

The state pays $2 for every $1 the association spends toward the actual cost of the project, up to a total of $175,000 per association. It is a reimbursement paid after the final inspection and the insurer's written confirmation of a credit.

Can we start the work before the grant is approved?

No. DFS says construction must not begin until the grant application is approved, and work done before approval won't qualify for grant funding.

What vote does the association need?

A majority vote of the board or of the total voting interests to take part, and for a grant that improves units, approval by at least 75 percent of the unit owners who reside in the building being improved, under s. 215.55871(2).

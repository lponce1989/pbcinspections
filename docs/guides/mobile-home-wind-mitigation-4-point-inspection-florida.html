Mobile homes: wind mitigation credits, tie-downs and 4-point inspections

Checked Nov 8, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: Citizens says its wind mitigation discounts are not offered for mobile homes. Florida law treats mobile home wind credits separately: s. 627.0629(3) says a rate filing for mobile home owner insurance must include appropriate discounts for mobile homes built to ANSI/ASCE 7-88, the standard HUD adopted on July 13, 1994, that also comply with state tie-down requirements, and s. 627.0629(7) says an insurer may offer up to 10 percent of the annual premium to owners who provide evidence of a current tie-down inspection. Citizens still requires a 4-point inspection for mobile home applications for properties more than 20 years old.

Wind mitigation discounts and the OIR-B1-1802

Citizens' FAQ on wind mitigation discounts says the discounts apply only to the wind portion of the policy premium and "are not offered for mobile homes." It tells policyholders to contact their agent to discuss what discounts are available for their home.

Citizens' wind mitigation inspection page lists the buildings its Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form (OIR-B1-1802) is for: single-family dwellings; one-to-four-unit residential dwellings, including apartments and condominiums; and residential buildings with one to three stories, including apartments and condominiums with five or more units. It lists a separate form, the MIT-BT II and III, for condominium buildings with four or more stories. Mobile homes are not on either list. For what the OIR-B1-1802 asks on a site-built house, see the wind mitigation form, question by question, and for condo buildings see condo wind mitigation and 4-point inspections.

The two mobile home credits in s. 627.0629

Section 627.0629(1), Florida Statutes, says a rate filing for residential property insurance must include actuarially reasonable discounts, credits or other rate differentials, or appropriate reductions in deductibles, for properties with fixtures or construction techniques demonstrated to reduce windstorm loss. The same section has two subsections written for mobile homes:

  • Subsection (3), HUD wind standard: a rate filing for mobile home owner insurance "must include appropriate discounts, credits, or other rate differentials" for mobile homes constructed to comply with American Society of Civil Engineers Standard ANSI/ASCE 7-88, adopted by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development on July 13, 1994, that also comply with all applicable tie-down requirements provided by state law.
  • Subsection (7), tie-down inspection: an insurer "may implement appropriate discounts or other rate differentials of up to 10 percent of the annual premium" to mobile home owners who provide evidence of a current inspection of tie-downs, certifying that the tie-downs have been properly installed and are in good condition.

Subsection (7) uses "may" and sets a ceiling of 10 percent. It does not name a form or say who must perform the inspection, so ask your insurer whether its filing includes the discount and what evidence it accepts.

The Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV) says Florida has wind zones II and III, and that mobile homes must be built in accordance with the standards that apply to the wind zone of the county where the home is to be set up, citing Rule 15C-1.01031, Florida Administrative Code.

Tie-down inspections and installation rules

Section 320.8325(1), Florida Statutes, directs the department to adopt uniform standards for installing mobile homes, manufactured homes and park trailers. It says the rules must ensure the home is installed on a permanent foundation that resists wind, flood, flotation, overturning, sliding and lateral movement, and that the owner is responsible for installation in accordance with department rules. Section 320.8325(2)(a) says insurers that insure these homes against windstorm damage "shall issue such insurance only if" the home has been installed in accordance with chapter 320 and department rules. Under s. 320.8325(2)(b), if an insured home sustains windstorm damage that indicates it was not installed as required, the insurer is not relieved of its policy obligations for that damage on the basis that the home was not properly installed.

Citizens' FAQ on its mobile home tie-down inspection says that if the inspection is refused, the policy will be cancelled or nonrenewed, and that Citizens will not order another tie-down inspection if the applicant refuses to allow the initial one.

FLHSMV says Chapter 15C-1, Florida Administrative Code, requires that all mobile and manufactured home installations be performed by a licensed mobile home installer, and its installer FAQ says a consumer cannot set up their own mobile home or park trailer. It also says all installation components and products must be certified by FLHSMV. FLHSMV's consumer page recommends having the tie-down system checked by a licensed mobile home installer, and lists these safety tips:

  • Check for loose straps, and make sure straps are properly aligned and not on an angle.
  • Check that the proper number of tie-downs have been installed properly.
  • Verify that ground anchors and stabilizer plates have been installed properly.
  • Be sure support piers are in contact with the frame.
  • Replace straps or ground anchors that show signs of corrosion or damage.

To be licensed, FLHSMV says a mobile home installer must attend a free 12-hour installation course and pass its exam, hold a $5,000 performance bond and carry $100,000 in general liability insurance. FLHSMV publishes a list of licensed mobile home installers on its installer page.

4-point and roof rules for mobile homes

Citizens' inspections page says Citizens "requires a four-point inspection for all property owner, dwelling and mobile home applications for properties more than 20 years old." A Florida-licensed inspector checks the electrical system, plumbing, heating and cooling (HVAC) and roof, and must provide the age, type and condition of each. Citizens says serious hazards or deficiencies, such as exposed wiring, active leaks or evidence of leaks, should be repaired before an application is submitted. See common 4-point inspection problems and fixes.

Citizens' roof age FAQ says documentation of full roof replacement is required for mobile homes with roofs more than 25 years old to be eligible for coverage. It notes that some exceptions apply and asks for documentation verifying the roof has at least five years of remaining useful life for underwriters to review. Florida's roof age law for homeowners' policies, s. 627.7011, says in subsection (6) that the section does not apply to mobile home policies. See Florida's roof age rules and Citizens' Roof Inspection Form (RCF-1).

The free state tie-down program

OIR's Wind Mitigation Resources page lists the Gulf Coast State College Mobile Home Tie-Down Program, managed by the Florida Division of Emergency Management. Gulf Coast State College says the Division's Hurricane Loss Mitigation Program funds the program through a contract with the college, which contracts with licensed mobile home installers to retrofit additional tie-downs to homes installed in 1999 and earlier. The college says:

  • The program is at no cost to the participant, and skirting removal and re-installation are included.
  • It does not intend to bring existing homes up to code, but makes the home as wind resistant as funding, physical characteristics and the condition of the premises allow.
  • Homes in a managed park or community with an HOA are served under the Park Application; the Individual Application is only for homes on privately owned land, and is not available to LLCs or investment firms.
  • Homeowners are given a Certificate of Completion after the work and any required post-inspections.
  • Because of the size of the waiting list, it could take several years before a park is serviced.

My Safe Florida Home is a different program. Section 215.5586, Florida Statutes, makes a hurricane mitigation inspection available only if the home is site-built and owner-occupied, among other conditions. See My Safe Florida Home vs. a private wind mitigation.

Before you book

  • Ask your insurer what it wants: Citizens does not offer its wind mitigation discounts for mobile homes, and s. 627.0629(7) leaves the tie-down discount to the insurer. Ask which report, if any, earns a credit on your policy and who it accepts as the inspector.
  • Check the home's age: Citizens requires a 4-point for mobile home applications for properties more than 20 years old, and roof documentation once a mobile home roof is more than 25 years old.
  • Use licensed people: FLHSMV says tie-down work must be done by a licensed mobile home installer. For a 4-point, Citizens wants a Florida-licensed inspector; see how to look up a license on DBPR.
  • Compare 4-point prices: 8 of the 16 companies we list publish a 4-point price. Listed prices run from $100 to $200, some of them starting prices, with a median of $150 counting each company once. Our listings don't record whether a company inspects mobile homes, so ask when you call. See the Palm Beach County prices page, or the Glades page, where Census estimates put mobile homes at about 13% of homes in Belle Glade and South Bay and 24% in Pahokee.

FAQ

Does Citizens give wind mitigation discounts on a mobile home?

No. Citizens' FAQ says its wind mitigation discounts apply only to the wind portion of the premium and are not offered for mobile homes.

Is there any Florida insurance discount for mobile home tie-downs?

Section 627.0629(7), Florida Statutes, says an insurer may implement discounts of up to 10 percent of the annual premium for mobile home owners who provide evidence of a current tie-down inspection certifying that the tie-downs are properly installed and in good condition. Separately, s. 627.0629(3) says mobile home owner rate filings must include appropriate discounts for homes built to ANSI/ASCE 7-88, adopted by HUD on July 13, 1994, that also comply with state tie-down requirements.

Does a mobile home need a 4-point inspection for Citizens?

Citizens says it requires a four-point inspection for all property owner, dwelling and mobile home applications for properties more than 20 years old.

What happens if I refuse Citizens' mobile home tie-down inspection?

Citizens says the policy will be cancelled or nonrenewed, and that it will not order another tie-down inspection if the applicant refuses the initial one.

Who can fix or add tie-downs on a mobile home in Florida?

FLHSMV says Chapter 15C-1, Florida Administrative Code, requires mobile and manufactured home installations to be performed by a licensed mobile home installer, and recommends having the tie-down system checked by one. The Gulf Coast State College program uses licensed installers to add tie-downs, at no cost, to homes installed in 1999 and earlier.

Can a mobile home get a free My Safe Florida Home inspection?

Section 215.5586, Florida Statutes, makes a My Safe Florida Home hurricane mitigation inspection available only if the home is site-built and owner-occupied, among other conditions.

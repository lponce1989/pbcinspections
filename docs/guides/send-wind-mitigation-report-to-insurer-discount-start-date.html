Sending your wind mitigation report to your insurer: who submits it and when the discount starts

Checked Nov 10, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation (OIR) says the homeowner should take the completed inspection form to their agent to begin processing the policy discounts. For Citizens Property Insurance, properly documented credits on a new policy apply from the start of coverage. Credits added in the middle of a policy term apply from the start of the current term for features built into the home, and from the date an inspector certifies them for later improvements, unless you can prove the improvements were already in place.

Who sends the form to your insurer

OIR's consumer page on hurricane loss mitigation discounts says you prove your home's features by having a licensed inspector examine it on the official Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form, and that the homeowner should then take the completed form to their agent to begin processing the discounts. Section 627.711(2)(a), Florida Statutes, says all insurers must use that uniform form when a policyholder submits it for wind insurance discounts.

The statute expects the form to come from more than one direction. Section 627.711(9) refers to a form "provided by a policyholder, a policyholder's agent, or an authorized mitigation inspector or inspection company." Citizens' March 19, 2026 agent bulletin on the revised form says the agent should review it to confirm the inspection is complete and all required photographs are attached before submission.

Citizens says an authorized representative can sign the form for the homeowner or applicant by writing "[Representative's signature] as authorized representative for [homeowner's or applicant's name]" on the signature line. Citizens also says an agent can sign the OIR-B1-1802 (Rev. 04/26) as the homeowner's or applicant's authorized representative. Representative signatures are not allowed on the Building Type II and III Mitigation Inspection Form used for condo buildings of four or more stories.

What Citizens checks before it accepts the form

Citizens' Wind Loss Mitigation Guide for the Rev. 04/26 form gives agents this checklist before applying for credits:

  • The correct mitigation inspection form is used. Citizens says acceptable forms vary by version date and building type.
  • The form is signed by a qualified inspector and by the customer.
  • The form is valid (within five years) and there have been no structural changes since the inspection.
  • The required photos for attributes 2 through 9 are complete.
  • Required product approval certificates, permits and any other documentation are included.

The guide says that if anything looks missing, inaccurate or inconsistent, the agent should contact the inspector to correct it before submitting to Citizens. It also says the address on the form must match the property address on the policy, that inspectors must initial and include the property address on each page, and that credits will not be granted if the form is completed by an unqualified or unlicensed inspector. See who can sign the form.

The form itself says at least one photograph or document must accompany it to validate each attribute marked in questions 2 through 9, and that the insurer may ask additional questions about the features it verifies. Every page tells you to "maintain a copy of this form and any documentation provided with the insurance policy."

Citizens' underwriters review all mitigation forms and supporting documents, and Citizens says a form may not be accepted if the appropriate professional did not perform the inspection or the professional's Florida license is not active or cannot be verified. For what happens if Citizens or another insurer wants a second look, see whether your insurer can re-inspect your report.

When the discount starts at Citizens

Citizens' informational notice IE #002-10, "Determining Effective Dates for Wind Mitigation Credits" (dated Feb 15, 2010; Citizens' page last updated Apr 16, 2026), sets these dates for properly documented credits:

Examples Citizens givesCredit applies from
New businessA new application with properly documented creditsThe inception date of coverage
Midterm, feature built into the homeRoof shape, or construction features required by the building code in force when the home was builtThe inception date of the current policy term
Midterm, later improvementOpening protection, secondary water resistance, or a replacement roof built to the Florida Building CodeThe date an inspector who meets Citizens' minimum qualifications certifies it
Midterm, later improvement with proof it was already therePaid receipts, executed work orders or building permits showing the improvement was in place since the current term beganMay be applied from the inception date of the current policy term

The notice says credits must be properly documented, and recommends inspection services that give detailed documentation such as photographs and building permit review or verification. It also says Citizens may verify the features by ordering an independent physical inspection, and that refusing to allow that inspection will result in the removal of wind mitigation credits and may result in coverage being discontinued.

If you are timing an upgrade, the practical point from the notice is to keep the paperwork. A permit or paid receipt is what Citizens lists as evidence that an improvement was in place earlier than the inspection date. If you need copies, see how to find your permits in Palm Beach County.

The notice and the Wind Loss Mitigation Guide are Citizens documents; the guide says its content applies only to Citizens' systems. If you are with another insurer, ask your agent how that company dates a new credit.

When Citizens may not need a new form

Citizens' Wind Loss Mitigation Guide says the Rev. 04/26 form is required for inspections completed on or after April 1, 2026, for homes upgraded or newly built with features that exceed Florida Building Code requirements when credits are requested, and when opening protection must be verified for Citizens' underwriting. It says the form may not be required when:

  • You already have a valid OIR-B1-1802 (Rev. 01/12) completed within five years of the inspection date.
  • You are applying only for a hip roof credit; clear photographs can be submitted instead of a full inspection. See roof shape on the form.
  • A valid completed roofing permit is available and only a roof cover credit is being requested.
  • The structure was built to the Florida Building Code on or after January 1, 2002, and no additional wind mitigation features exist.

The same guide notes that the OIR-B1-1802 is used only to verify wind mitigation construction features and is not acceptable proof of roof condition or replacement. Roof condition is a separate report; see Citizens' Roof Inspection Form (RCF-1).

Bought the house? Citizens' two answers

Citizens' sources read differently on a prior owner's form. The Wind Loss Mitigation Guide says that if the property is a new purchase, the form may be in the name of and signed by the prior owner, and that the current homeowner, previous homeowner or an authorized representative must sign it.

Citizens' FAQ "Do I have to get a new mitigation inspection if the previous owner has a valid inspection form?" (updated Oct 21, 2026) answers yes. It says you must provide a new form because the current property owner must be present and attest to the inspection, quotes the homeowner statement on the form, and notes that mitigation credits cannot be transferred from a prior owner. Ask your agent which rule Citizens will apply before you count on the seller's form; for more on buying, see using the seller's wind mitigation report.

If the discount doesn't show up

  • Check your discount notice: s. 627.711(1) requires your insurer, on an OIR-prescribed form, to notify you when the policy is issued and at each renewal of the availability and range of each wind mitigation discount, credit or deductible reduction. The Department of Financial Services (DFS) says admitted insurers must send this Notice of Premium Discounts for Hurricane Loss Mitigation with each new residential policy and at each renewal.
  • Look at the right part of the premium: the OIR-B1-1655 notice says the discounts apply only to the hurricane-wind portion of your premium and are not cumulative, so the total is not the sum of the individual discounts. See how much a wind mitigation inspection saves.
  • Ask the company first: DFS asks you to contact the insurance company and give it the chance to resolve the issue before filing with DFS, and to keep detailed records of your communications.
  • Then contact DFS: OIR says that if your agent or insurance company refuses to accept inspection results submitted on the proper form, you can file a complaint or speak with an insurance specialist at the DFS Division of Consumer Services, toll-free at (877) 693-5236 or (850) 413-3089 from out of state. DFS asks for the company name, your policy number, your declarations page, a description of the concern and supporting documents, and says that by statute companies have 14 days to respond to the Department once a concern is filed.

Before you book

  • Gather your paperwork first: Citizens asks you to give the inspector copies of mitigation documents such as receipts with product specifications, work orders and invoices, roofing permits and notice-of-acceptance letters. See how to prepare for the inspection.
  • Check the license: look up the inspector on DBPR; our DBPR guide shows how.
  • Compare prices: 10 of the 16 companies we list publish a wind mitigation price. Listed prices run from $100 to $200, some of them starting prices, with a median of $150 counting each company once. See the Palm Beach County prices page or find an inspector on the city pages.

FAQ

Who sends my wind mitigation report to my insurance company?

Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation says the homeowner should take the completed inspection form to their agent to begin processing the policy discounts. Section 627.711(9) also refers to forms provided by a policyholder, a policyholder's agent, or an inspector or inspection company.

When does a Citizens wind mitigation credit start?

On a new policy, Citizens applies properly documented credits from the inception date of coverage. Added midterm, credits for features built into the home apply from the start of the current term, and credits for later improvements apply from the date an inspector certifies them.

Can I get a Citizens credit backdated for shutters or a new roof I already had?

Citizens' notice IE #002-10 says that if a properly documented inspection comes with enough evidence, such as paid receipts, executed work orders or building permits, that the improvements were in place since the current term began, the credits may be applied from the start of that term.

Can my insurance agent sign the wind mitigation form for me?

Citizens says an agent can sign the OIR-B1-1802 (Rev. 04/26) as an authorized representative of the homeowner or applicant. Representative signatures are not allowed on the Building Type II and III Mitigation Inspection Form.

What if my insurer won't accept my wind mitigation form?

OIR says that if your agent or insurance company refuses to accept inspection results submitted on the proper form, you can contact the DFS Division of Consumer Services at (877) 693-5236, or (850) 413-3089 from out of state. DFS asks you to contact the company first and give it a chance to resolve the issue.

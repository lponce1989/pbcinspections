Roof or hurricane damage claim: deadlines, the adjuster's inspection and mediation

Checked Nov 15, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: under Florida Statutes s. 627.70132, notice of a new or reopened property insurance claim must be given to your insurer within 1 year after the date of loss, and notice of a supplemental claim within 18 months. For a hurricane, the date of loss is the date the hurricane made landfall. Under s. 627.70131, your insurer must acknowledge the claim within 7 days, conduct any physical inspection within 30 days after it receives your proof-of-loss statements, and pay or deny the claim within 60 days after notice, unless factors beyond its control apply. If you disagree with the result, the Department of Financial Services (DFS) runs a mediation program whose cost the insurer pays.

This guide covers the inspection your insurer's adjuster does after damage. For the inspections you order yourself for coverage or discounts, see 4-point vs. wind mitigation vs. roof certification. For inspections your insurer orders before writing or renewing a policy, see insurer inspection found problems.

Deadlines to report the claim

Section 627.70132 sets these time limits for property insurance claims, for loss or damage caused by any peril:

  • New or reopened claim: barred unless notice was given to the insurer, as the policy requires, within 1 year after the date of loss. A reopened claim is one the insurer closed and then reopened at your request for additional costs for damage you had already disclosed.
  • Supplemental claim: barred unless notice was given within 18 months after the date of loss. A supplemental claim is for additional damage from the same peril that the insurer already adjusted, or for costs incurred while completing repairs under an open claim.
  • Weather events: for hurricanes, tornadoes, windstorms, severe rain and other weather-related events, the date of loss is the date the hurricane made landfall or the date the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration verified the event.
  • Servicemembers: the time limits are tolled during deployment to a combat zone or combat support posting that materially affects a named insured servicemember's ability to file.
  • Condo loss assessments: notice of a claim under loss assessment coverage must be given within 1 year after the date of loss or within 90 days after the association votes to levy the assessment, whichever is later, and no later than 3 years after the date of loss.

Citizens Property Insurance tells its customers not to wait: report a claim as soon as you become aware of or suspect damage, even if you don't know the full extent of it. Citizens takes claims 24/7 at (866) 411-2742 or online through myPolicy.

What your insurer must do, and when

Section 627.70131 applies to residential property insurers, and the Homeowner Claims Bill of Rights in Florida Statutes s. 627.7142 summarizes these rights in plain terms. Your insurer must send you that Bill of Rights within 14 days after it first hears from you about a claim.

  • 7 days to acknowledge: the insurer must review and acknowledge a communication about a claim within 7 calendar days. Unless it tells you the claim appears not to be covered, the acknowledgment of a new claim must include the claim forms, instructions and a telephone number.
  • 7 days to start investigating: unless the policy or law says otherwise, the insurer must begin its investigation within 7 days after it receives your proof-of-loss statements.
  • 30 days to inspect: if the investigation involves a physical inspection, it must happen within 30 days after the insurer receives the proof-of-loss statements. The adjuster assigned must give you a printed or electronic document with his or her name and state adjuster license number, and later messages about the claim must include the name and license number of the adjuster sending them.
  • Photos, video and drones: the insurer may investigate electronically, for example with photos, video, video calls with you, or drone footage, and may let you use those methods to help. It can still send a licensed adjuster to inspect in person.
  • Copy of the estimate: the insurer must send you any detailed estimate of the loss within 7 days after its adjuster generates it.
  • Coverage answer on request: the Bill of Rights says that, if you ask in writing, the insurer must tell you within 30 days after you submit a complete proof-of-loss statement whether the claim is covered in full, partly covered or denied, or that it is still being investigated.
  • 60 days to pay or deny: the insurer must pay or deny the claim, or a portion of it, within 60 days after it receives notice of an initial, reopened or supplemental claim, and explain the decision in writing. A payment made after that bears interest from the date the insurer received notice of the claim.

These deadlines can move. Section 627.70131 allows extra time for factors beyond the insurer's control, such as a Governor's state of emergency for which the Office of Insurance Regulation issues an order, and limits any extension of the time to pay or deny to 30 more days. The deadlines are also paused during DFS mediation, and when you don't provide material claim information the insurer requested within 10 days of receiving the request.

The adjuster's inspection

Citizens' claims pages describe what happens after you report a claim:

  • Scheduling: a Citizens adjuster contacts you to discuss the loss and schedule a property-loss inspection, where the adjuster reviews the loss and collects photos, receipts and estimates.
  • Getting ready: copy and label your damage photos, collect and copy your temporary repair receipts, keep damaged items that may have contributed to the claim, find your mortgage loan number and mortgagee clause, and put pets out of the way.
  • Check who shows up: Citizens says to ask for and check the adjuster's ID when the adjuster arrives. DFS's Licensee Search lets you look up adjuster licenses by name or license number.
  • Experts: Citizens says an engineer or specialized contractor may sometimes inspect the damage with its adjusters, and that you are welcome to get your own contractor's estimate for the adjuster to review.
  • After the inspection: a claims examiner reviews your policy for coverage and deductibles. If the covered damage is more than your deductible, the claim goes to payment. If the damage is not covered, Citizens says it will contact you and send a written report explaining the decision.

For how a hurricane deductible is figured and when it applies, see Florida hurricane deductibles and your wind mitigation.

Emergency repairs before the adjuster comes

Citizens lists turning off broken water supply lines, boarding up broken windows, putting a tarp on a damaged roof and removing standing water as examples of emergency repairs that may be needed to prevent further damage. It also says the adjuster must inspect your home and authorize any permanent tear-out, removal or repair before that work is done.

The Homeowner Claims Bill of Rights advises you to file claims directly with your insurer, check with your insurer before signing a repair contract to confirm any managed repair or preferred vendor provisions, make and document the emergency repairs needed to prevent further damage, keep the damaged property if feasible, keep all receipts, and take photos or video of the damage before and after repairs. Citizens asks you to keep receipts for temporary repairs and for any additional living expenses while your home is unlivable after a covered loss, and to give the originals to your adjuster.

Roofers who offer free inspections

Florida Statutes s. 489.147 limits what contractors can do around roof claims. A contractor may not:

  • Offer you a rebate, gift, gift card, cash, coupon, waiver of your insurance deductible or any other thing of value in exchange for letting the contractor inspect your roof or for making an insurance claim for roof damage.
  • Solicit you with a door hanger, flyer, business card, email or other ad that encourages a roof damage claim unless it states, in type of at least 12 points and at least half the size of its largest type, that you are responsible for your deductible and that paying, waiving or rebating a deductible, or filing a claim with false information, is insurance fraud punishable as a third-degree felony.
  • Interpret your policy, advise you on your coverage or adjust your claim, unless the contractor is also a licensed public adjuster.
  • Give you an agreement authorizing repairs without a good faith, itemized estimate of the cost of services and materials.

A contractor can be fined up to $10,000 per violation. A roof contract must include a notice that the contractor may not offer those inducements, and if it doesn't, you may void the contract within 10 days after signing. If you sign a roof repair or replacement contract within 180 days of an event covered by a Governor's state of emergency for your area, you may cancel it without penalty within 10 days or by the official start date, whichever comes first, by certified mail or another mailing method with proof.

Under Florida Statutes s. 627.7152(13), post-loss benefits under a residential property policy issued on or after Jan 1, 2023 cannot be assigned, so an assignment of benefits to a contractor under such a policy is void. The Bill of Rights advises confirming a contractor's Florida license, and any complaints against it, with the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR), and requiring proof of insurance before repairs begin. Citizens lists asking a vendor to inflate a repair estimate or invoice by the deductible amount as a form of property insurance fraud.

Public adjuster fees

If you hire a public adjuster, Florida Statutes s. 626.854(11) caps the fee at 10% of claim payments for claims based on an event covered by a Governor's state of emergency, for claims made during the year after the declaration, and at 20% for other claims. For a reopened or supplemental claim, the fee can be based only on the new payments the public adjuster obtains, and may not exceed 20% of them.

If you disagree with the decision

Under s. 627.7015, your insurer must tell you about the DFS mediation program when a policy is issued or renewed, or when you file a claim. DFS describes residential property mediation as a pre-appraisal, pre-suit meeting with your insurer and a certified, neutral mediator:

  • When: DFS requires a claim determination from the insurer, including an offer to pay, before a dispute can be mediated, and asks you to give the company a chance to resolve the issue first.
  • Which disputes: $500 or more after the deductible, about what caused the damage or the amount offered to repair it. DFS cannot mediate National Flood Insurance Program policies, liability coverage, claims already in litigation or appraisal, claims the insurer suspects involve fraud, or a cause of loss the policy doesn't cover.
  • Cost: the insurer pays the entire $350 cost, unless you miss the conference and want it rescheduled, in which case you pay $350 for the new one.
  • Timing: the conference is to take place within 21 days of the mediator's assignment.
  • Result: mediation is nonbinding. If you reach a settlement, you have 3 business days to rescind it, as long as you have not cashed the check and you tell the company.
  • Sinkholes: the Bill of Rights lists neutral evaluation for disputed sinkhole claims that your policy covers.

To request mediation or get help with any claim, contact the DFS Division of Consumer Services online or at (877) 693-5236, or (850) 413-5818 from out of state.

After the repairs: proof and new inspections

For property damage repairs you do yourself, Citizens' FAQ asks for photos of the repairs plus receipts showing the work was paid in full, an inspection report or permit showing the work was completed, or a final roof permit if the roof was replaced. For roof repairs, underwriting may ask for a Roof Inspection Form; see what's on Citizens' Roof Inspection Form (RCF-1). If the claim pays for a new roof, new roof: Florida reroof rules and your wind mitigation covers which wind mitigation questions it changes. Our Palm Beach County prices page lists the prices inspectors publish, and the city pages list inspectors by area.

Under Florida Statutes s. 627.4133(3), a claim that results from an act of God may not be used as a reason to cancel or nonrenew your policy unless the insurer can show, by claims frequency or otherwise, that you failed to take action it reasonably requested to prevent the damage from happening again.

FAQ

How long do I have to file a hurricane damage claim in Florida?

Under Florida Statutes s. 627.70132, notice of a new or reopened claim must be given to your insurer within 1 year after the date of loss, and notice of a supplemental claim within 18 months. For a hurricane, the date of loss is the date it made landfall.

How long does my insurer have to inspect the damage?

Section 627.70131 says an insurer must conduct any physical inspection within 30 days after it receives your proof-of-loss statements. The adjuster must give you his or her name and state adjuster license number in writing or electronically.

How long does my insurer have to pay or deny the claim?

60 days after it receives notice of the claim, under s. 627.70131(7), unless factors beyond its control apply. Late payments bear interest from the date the insurer received notice of the claim.

Can a roofer offer to cover my deductible or give me a gift for a free roof inspection?

No. Florida Statutes s. 489.147 bars contractors from offering a rebate, gift, cash, deductible waiver or any other thing of value in exchange for inspecting your roof or making a roof damage claim, with fines of up to $10,000 per violation.

Does DFS mediation cost me anything?

DFS says the insurer pays the entire $350 cost of residential property mediation, unless you miss the conference and want it rescheduled. Mediation is nonbinding, and you have 3 business days to rescind a settlement if you haven't cashed the check.

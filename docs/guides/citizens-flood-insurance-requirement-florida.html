Citizens flood insurance requirement: who needs it, the 2027 deadline and your flood zone

Checked Nov 17, 2026

Not insurance advice. General information only. Your insurer or agent decides what it needs.

Short answer: under Florida Statutes s. 627.351(6)(aa), Citizens Property Insurance requires flood insurance as a condition of coverage for personal lines residential policies. Citizens says its policies that include wind coverage need a flood policy if the home is in a FEMA special flood hazard area, whatever its value. Outside that area, the requirement applies in 2026 when the dwelling value (Coverage A) is $400,000 or more, and from Jan 1, 2027 to all policies regardless of value. Condominium unit-owner policies, tenant content policies and policies that exclude windstorm or hail coverage are not required to buy flood insurance.

Florida's required seller flood disclosure, in s. 689.302, states that "homeowners' insurance policies do not include coverage for damage resulting from floods." This guide covers who has to buy that separate coverage to keep a Citizens policy, how much is enough, and how to look up your flood zone. For hurricane (wind) deductibles, see Florida hurricane deductibles and your wind mitigation.

Who has to carry flood insurance

Citizens' flood page splits policies by whether the home is inside FEMA's special flood hazard area (SFHA):

  • Inside the SFHA: personal residential policies with wind coverage for homes in the SFHA (for example zones A, AO, AH, A1-A30, AE, A99, V, V1-V30 and VE) must have and maintain a flood policy. The statute required this at issuance for new policies issued on or after April 1, 2023, and by renewal for policies renewing on or after July 1, 2023.
  • Outside the SFHA, $600,000 or more: required for policies effective on or after Jan 1, 2024.
  • Outside the SFHA, $500,000 or more: required for policies effective on or after Jan 1, 2025.
  • Outside the SFHA, $400,000 or more: required for policies effective on or after Jan 1, 2026.
  • Everyone else: the statute extends the requirement to all other personal lines residential property Citizens insures for policies effective on or after Jan 1, 2027. Citizens words this as "all policies, regardless of value."

Citizens says the thresholds outside the SFHA are measured against the structure's current Coverage A (dwelling) amount, and that the requirement applies to new and renewing policies. Citizens' flood page cites s. 627.715, and the phase-in schedule itself is in s. 627.351(6)(aa).

Who is exempt

  • No wind coverage: the statute says flood insurance is not required to keep a Citizens policy that does not cover the peril of wind. Citizens' page describes these as policies that exclude windstorm or hail coverage.
  • Condo units: the statute exempts policies written on a condominium unit owners form, and Citizens lists condominium unit-owner policies as not required to buy flood insurance.
  • Renters: Citizens' page also lists tenant content policies as not required to buy flood insurance. The statute's list of exemptions names only the first two.

How much flood coverage counts

  • Dwelling limit: Citizens says flood coverage on a dwelling policy must be equal to or greater than your Citizens policy dwelling value (Coverage A).
  • NFIP cap: if that limit is not available from the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), Citizens says it will accept the maximum amount you are eligible for, which it lists as $250,000 Coverage A under the regular program and $35,000 under the emergency program.
  • Private flood policies: the statute says the flood insurance must meet, at a minimum, the dwelling coverage available from the NFIP or the requirements of s. 627.715(1)(a)1., 2. and 3. Those three subparagraphs describe standard flood insurance (the same coverage as a standard NFIP policy), preferred flood insurance (standard coverage plus some outside water intrusion, additional living expenses and replacement-cost contents claims) and customized flood insurance (coverage broader than standard). Flexible and supplemental flood insurance, in subparagraphs 4. and 5. of s. 627.715(1)(a), are not on that list.

The proof Citizens asks for

Citizens says new applicants and renewing policyholders must send proof of flood coverage and a completed Policyholder Affirmation Regarding Flood Insurance (CIT FW01). The statute calls for a form approved by the Office of Insurance Regulation affirming that Citizens does not provide flood insurance and that, if you do not get it from another insurer, the risk will not be eligible for Citizens coverage. Citizens accepts any one of these as proof:

  • A copy of the submitted flood application and proof of payment, as initial proof if the application is still pending.
  • A copy of the flood policy declarations page.
  • For Citizens Wind-Only policies, proof of a flood endorsement that meets the minimum standard on the underlying multiperil policy.

The statute says Citizens may deny coverage to an applicant or policyholder who refuses to secure and maintain flood insurance.

Where the dwelling value comes from

Citizens' coverages page says Coverage A covers the building and any attached structures, and that a replacement cost amount is the cost of completely rebuilding the home after a total loss, which may be greater than its appraised real estate value. Under s. 627.351(6)(ff), Citizens must accept a replacement cost valuation completed within the 12 months before the application or renewal date from any of these sources, and must use the lowest valuation as the insured value of the dwelling, excluding land value:

  • Replacement cost valuation software designed for setting insurance replacement costs, with an itemized calculation of the cost of reconstruction.
  • A certified or licensed real estate appraiser's valuation formulated to establish insurance replacement cost rather than market value, with an itemized calculation.
  • A valuation by a licensed general, building or residential contractor or a licensed professional engineer, with an itemized calculation of the total price of reconstruction.

Finding your flood zone in Palm Beach County

  • What the SFHA is: FEMA defines the special flood hazard area as the area shown on a Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM) as Zone A, AO, A1-A30, AE, A99, AH, the AR zones, V1-V30, VE or V, and says it is where the mandatory purchase of flood insurance applies.
  • FEMA's map: the FEMA Flood Map Service Center lets you search the flood map by address.
  • The county's lookup: Palm Beach County's Office of Emergency Management has a Flood Zone Look-up Tool on its Know Your Zone page, and asks you not to use punctuation when typing the address.
  • Not your evacuation zone: the same county page separates the two. It describes an evacuation zone as an area that may be inundated by storm surge, and a flood zone as an area at risk of flooding such as poor drainage, sheet flow or coastal tides.
  • Which map is current: the county's Planning, Zoning and Building GIS quick reference says its "Flood Zone 2024" layer shows the current effective FIRMs revised on 12/20/2024, and that the "Flood Zones 2017" layer shows the former FIRMs adopted on 10/5/2017.
  • More than one zone: the same quick reference notes that sometimes multiple flood zones affect one property, and says to click on portions of the property to see the zone for each part.

Buying a home with a Citizens policy

Under s. 689.302, the seller of residential property must complete and give the buyer a flood disclosure at or before the time the sales contract is signed. The form says whether the seller knows of flooding that damaged the property during the seller's ownership, has filed a flood damage claim with an insurer (including the NFIP), and has received flood damage assistance (including from FEMA). It also encourages the buyer to discuss separate flood insurance with an insurance agent. For the inspection reports a buyer may need for the wind side of the policy, see buying a Florida home: can you use the seller's wind mitigation report?

FAQ

Does Citizens require flood insurance in 2026?

Yes, for many policies. Citizens says personal residential policies with wind coverage need flood insurance if the home is in a FEMA special flood hazard area, and outside that area if the dwelling value (Coverage A) is $400,000 or more for policies effective on or after Jan 1, 2026.

When does every Citizens policyholder need flood insurance?

Under s. 627.351(6)(aa), the requirement extends to all other personal lines residential property Citizens insures for policies effective on or after Jan 1, 2027, unless the policy is exempt.

Do Citizens condo unit owners need flood insurance?

No. The statute exempts policies written on a condominium unit owners form, and Citizens lists condominium unit-owner policies, tenant content policies and policies that exclude windstorm or hail coverage as not required to buy flood insurance.

How much flood coverage does Citizens require?

Citizens says the flood dwelling limit must be equal to or greater than your Citizens Coverage A. If that is not available from the NFIP, it accepts the maximum you are eligible for, which it lists as $250,000 under the regular program and $35,000 under the emergency program.

How do I find my flood zone in Palm Beach County?

Use FEMA's Flood Map Service Center search by address, or the Flood Zone Look-up Tool on Palm Beach County's Know Your Zone page. The county's GIS guide says the current effective flood maps were revised on 12/20/2024.
